Overview

Priya Nakamura is standing in the loading dock at 6:15 in the morning, watching a forklift lower the last crate of FlowState demo units onto a truck bound for the convention center, when her phone buzzes with the subject line she's been dreading: "Board call moved up — need your channel plan by end of day."

She should feel good. The bottle works. The cartridge clicks in, reads the wearer's fitness tracker over Bluetooth, and doses the electrolyte-and-flavor mix before anyone even feels thirsty. Investors love the demo. What she doesn't have yet is a launch.

Two folders sit on the passenger seat of her car, each one a complete media plan against the same fixed advertising budget, each one already approved and ready to submit. One leans hard into television and radio — loud, broad, hard to miss. The other leans into search and a single fitness-magazine placement — narrower, but aimed at people already looking for exactly this. Solstice Labs gets one shot at the trade show, and one number on the board slide afterward: how many of the people they reached actually became paying subscribers.

Her manufacturing lead taps on the car window. The truck is loaded and pulling out. The board call is in nine hours.

Priya picks up both folders, one in each hand, and does not open either one yet.

Background

Priya Nakamura is the VP of Marketing at Solstice Labs, a small consumer-hardware startup. She is personally accountable to the board for the result of Solstice Labs' first product launch: FlowState, a reusable smart bottle whose snap-in cartridge automatically doses electrolytes and flavor based on the wearer's paired fitness-tracker activity and sweat-rate data, rather than a fixed pre-mixed recipe. The company's pitch is simple — "the first bottle that adjusts your hydration mix to what your body is actually doing" — and Priya's job is to turn a fixed launch budget into as many paying subscribers as it can buy.

Before launch, Solstice Labs ran a pre-launch survey and pilot that measured an AIDA-style conversion funnel — awareness, interest, trial, and paid adoption — across two population segments the company is targeting: Segment 1, student athletes ages 13–22, and Segment 2, working professionals ages 25–45 (see Appendix B). Five advertising channels are available for the launch, each with a real, researched cost benchmark and its own demographic skew: SEO/paid search, a sports/fitness-focused cable TV package, a general daytime/primetime broadcast TV package, drive-time radio, and a full-page placement in a health/fitness magazine (see Appendix A).

Priya's team has pre-built exactly two allocation plans against Solstice Labs' fixed launch advertising budget (see Appendix C), and she must submit exactly one to the board before the trade-show deadline. The Broadcast Blitz puts the majority of the budget into the two TV packages and radio — mass reach, broad demographics, a channel mix built for raw awareness. The Precision Funnel puts the majority of the budget into SEO and the magazine placement — narrower reach, aimed at an audience that is already looking for a product like this one. Whichever plan Priya submits governs the entire launch budget; there is no blended plan and no second submission once the trade-show window closes.

The funnel conversion rates that translate a channel's audience into paying subscribers are documented in Appendix D, along with a note on which of those rates come from real published industry benchmarks and which are modeling assumptions specific to Solstice Labs' own product and pilot (see Appendix E for the full assumption list, each with its basis). Every sourced figure in this case is documented in Appendix F. The vocabulary and "how to…" skills this case will ask of you are listed in Appendix G — that is where Challenge 1 sends you.

She has to submit one form.

Challenges

  1. Research and find an online resource that answers one of the "how to…" questions from Appendix G. Post a link to the resource as your submission.
  2. Post 250 or more "excellent" words pitching a firm stance to your audience on which you would choose and why. Your pitch must include: (a) use of the "how to…" resource you researched in Challenge 1; (b) at least one original visual display of the data.
  3. Your pitch must include:
    • D1 — A segmented funnel table for both plans: for every channel and every segment, chain the percentages together — audience reached, times the segment's share of that audience, times the awareness rate, times the interest-given-awareness rate, times the trial-given-interest rate, times the adoption-given-trial rate — down to a projected number of paying subscribers. Show every intermediate percent step; do not skip from reach straight to subscribers. Appendix D does not give you an awareness rate or an interest rate for the print/magazine channel — state and justify your own values for those two rates before you can finish this table.
    • D2 — A reach-per-dollar and cost-per-acquisition table for every channel in both plans: how many people does a dollar reach in each channel, and once you have D1's subscriber totals, what does each plan actually pay per paying subscriber it acquires?
    • D3 — A weighted-total comparison: the total projected paying subscribers for Plan A and for Plan B, found by summing D1's channel-by-segment results, and the percent margin between them.
    • D4 — A budget-shift system of equations. Group the five channels into a broadcast-leaning bucket (the two TV packages plus radio) and a precision-leaning bucket (SEO plus print). Using the per-dollar subscriber yield of each bucket, set up a system of two linear equations and solve it to find the exact dollar split that would make the two plans produce the same number of subscribers. Show your work algebraically, not just by guess-and-check.
    • D5 — A one-paragraph written recommendation that names which plan you are submitting, states the reach-per-dollar and cost-per-acquisition case for it from D2, and explicitly addresses how many dollars of reallocation (from D4) it would take to flip your recommendation — and whether you think that reallocation is realistic.
  4. An appendix showing evidence of your original thinking, work, calculations and processes you used to complete the challenges (pictures, screenshots, notes, etc.). Your work in the appendix does not count toward your word count.
  5. Participate twice or more in the Case Study discussion. If you are absent the day of a discussion post 500 "excellent" words on the questions above.

    Working Notebook

    This is a calculator, not an answer key. Type your own numbers first — then hit Check to see if your arithmetic matches. It won't tell you which plan to recommend; that part is still yours.

    Step 1 — D1: TV channels' segmented funnel, Plan A

    Using Appendix A/D's rates, chain reach × segment share × awareness × interest × trial-conversion × adoption for TV–Sports and TV–General under Plan A's dollar amounts, for each segment separately.

    D1 also asks for the full table (all 5 channels, both plans) and your own justified print awareness/interest rate — this calculator only checks the two fully-determined TV channels above.

    Step 2 — D2: Reach-per-dollar and channel-level cost-per-acquisition

    Reach-per-dollar and a channel's own cost-per-acquisition don't depend on which plan you're looking at — only on that channel's own CPM/CPC and funnel rates. Compute both for TV–Sports and for SEO.

    Step 3 — D3: Plan totals and margin

    Sum D1's channel-by-segment results across all five channels for each plan, then find the percent margin between the two totals. (Uses this calculator's own reference print-channel assumption — see the note below.)

    This calculator's plan totals use one specific, disclosed reference value for the print channel's awareness/interest rate so there's something concrete to check your arithmetic against: 15% awareness, 12% interest (this calculator's own reference assumption only — not given anywhere in Appendix D). Challenge 3 (D1) asks you to justify your own value — a different, equally well-justified value will legitimately produce a different total, and that's fine as long as you show your reasoning.

    Step 4 — D4: Broadcast-bucket / precision-bucket system of equations

    Broadcast bucket = TV–Sports + TV–General + Radio. Precision bucket = SEO + Print. Using Plan A's own broadcast-bucket mix and Plan B's own precision-bucket mix, find each bucket's per-dollar subscriber yield, then solve x + y = $60,000 and (broadcast yield)·x + (precision yield)·y = Plan B's total for the break-even split.

    Still just checking your own arithmetic — it will not tell you which plan to recommend. That's your D5 pitch to make. If you've revealed and applied the Twist below, this step re-checks against the +50% TV-CPM scenario instead.
