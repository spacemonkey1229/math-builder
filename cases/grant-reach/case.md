## Overview

The printer in the corner of Deja Whitfield's office has been running since 7 a.m., and it still isn't done.

She pulls the newest stack anyway, taps it square, and checks the top sheet against the spreadsheet glowing on her second monitor. Reminder packet. FAFSA renewal deadline. GPA verification deadline. Same two dates, printed in bold, on every single page. Outside her cracked door she can hear the financial aid lobby filling up — it's March, and every March the same thing happens: a wave of students who were fully funded last spring show up in April having quietly lost it all, because a form nobody reminded them about slipped past a deadline nobody reminded them about.

Her director stopped by twenty minutes ago with the number that's still sitting on Deja's desk: this year's mailing budget, final, not one dollar more. Deja has two lists open in two tabs. One is long. One is short. The long one costs money the office doesn't have much of. The short one is free — literally free, funded by a state allowance that covers exactly the students it's built for and not one more.

She minimizes the spreadsheet. On her desk are two mail-house order forms, both filled out, both ready to submit. She has to pick one before the print job even finishes.

## Background

Deja Whitfield is the Financial Aid Outreach Coordinator at Cascade State University, a mid-size public university in California. Her job is to make sure continuing students who currently receive need-based grant aid actually complete the paperwork to keep it — a federal FAFSA renewal and, for state-grant recipients, an annual GPA verification with the California Student Aid Commission (CSAC). Students who miss either deadline don't get an extension; they lose the aid for the following term (SEE APPENDIX A).

Cascade State's aid-renewal population this cycle is **9,000 continuing undergraduates** who received either a federal Pell Grant, a state Cal Grant, or both, in the current academic year (SEE APPENDIX A). Some of these students are eligible for the Pell Grant only, some for the state's Cal Grant only, and some for both — Deja's office tracks all three groups separately because the two programs use different eligibility rules: Pell Grant eligibility is based on a federal need calculation with no single income cutoff, while California's Cal Grant program publishes a flat family-income ceiling that varies by household size (SEE APPENDIX A). A student can clear one gate and not the other.

Every student who gets a printed reminder packet in the mail is measurably more likely to complete their renewal on time than a student who gets nothing (SEE APPENDIX D). Deja's office has budget for exactly one mailing decision this cycle:

**Order A — The Full Reach:**
Mail a reminder packet to every student in the **union** of Pell-eligible and state-grant-eligible students — everyone eligible for at least one of the two programs. This is the larger list. There is no state funding mechanism that covers a merged list like this one; every piece is billed to Cascade State's own outreach budget, at the university's commercial mail rate (SEE APPENDIX C).

**Order B — The Shared Only:**
Mail a reminder packet only to the **intersection** — students who are eligible for both programs at once. CSAC provides participating campuses a small administrative mailing allowance specifically for this shared-eligibility group, and it fully covers the cost. Order B spends **zero dollars** of Cascade State's own budget. Every student who is Pell-eligible only, or state-eligible only, receives no packet under this order.

Cascade State's total discretionary outreach mailing budget for the year — covering this mailing and everything else Deja's office sends — is **$1,600** (SEE APPENDIX A).

Appendix A defines the two eligibility sets and the population counts Deja is working from. Appendix B gives the Venn diagram groundwork. Appendix C is the university's actual commercial mail rate table. Appendix D is what's known — and what has to be assumed — about how much a mailed reminder actually moves a student's odds of renewing on time. Appendix E gives the dollar value of the aid itself, for context. Appendix F lists every source. Appendix G is where Challenge 1 sends you.

She has to submit one order.

## Appendix A — Eligibility Sets and the Renewal Population

Cascade State's aid-renewal population is fixed at **9,000 continuing undergraduates** for this case (a stated scenario scale — no public roster of a real university's dual-eligibility population exists; see Modeling Assumptions below).

**Federal Pell Grant eligibility.** Nationwide, **32.4% of undergraduates** were awarded a Pell Grant in AY2023-24 [S1]. Pell eligibility is computed from a federal need formula (the Student Aid Index), not a single income cutoff — a student's family size, assets, and number of household members in college all factor in, which is why Pell eligibility and a flat state income ceiling don't sort students the same way.

**California Cal Grant eligibility.** California's Cal Grant program publishes flat income ceilings by household size and grant type (SEE TABLE A-1). For a household of four, the 2025-26 ceiling is **$135,900** for Cal Grant A/C and **$71,500** for Cal Grant B [S3] — both well below the range where most Pell awards phase out, which is why a large majority of Cal-Grant-eligible students are also Pell-eligible, but not all of them (asset limits, dependency status, and household-size adjustments differ between the two programs).

**Table A-1 — 2025-26 Cal Grant Income Ceilings by Household Size [S3]**

| Household size | Cal Grant A & C | Cal Grant B |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | $122,100 | $57,000 |
| 3 | $125,100 | $64,200 |
| 4 | $135,900 | $71,500 |
| 5 | $145,700 | $79,900 |
| 6 or more | $157,100 | $86,300 |

## Appendix B — Venn Diagram Groundwork

Applying Appendix A and the Modeling Assumptions above:

| Region | Set expression | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Pell-eligible only | Pell − State | 1,580 |
| State-eligible only | State − Pell | 334 |
| Both (the intersection) | Pell ∩ State | 1,336 |
| **Union (either or both)** | **Pell ∪ State** | **3,250** |
| Neither | outside Pell ∪ State | 5,750 |

Check: |Pell| = 1,580 + 1,336 = 2,916 (matches 32.4% of 9,000 [S1]). |State| = 334 + 1,336 = 1,670 (matches MA2). |Pell ∪ State| = |Pell| + |State| − |Pell ∩ State| = 2,916 + 1,670 − 1,336 = 3,250.

A blank, unlabeled two-circle Venn diagram template for D1 is available at: https://www.canva.com/graphs/venn-diagrams/ (if this link is unavailable, any hand-drawn two-circle diagram with the four regions above satisfies the deliverable — the data above is what's load-bearing, not the specific tool).

## Appendix C — Cascade State's Commercial Mail Rate

Cascade State's mail house bills at USPS commercial Marketing Mail letter rates. USPS publishes three automation presort tiers, which reward a mailer for pre-sorting by ZIP code — the deeper the sort, the cheaper the piece (SEE TABLE C-1). Cascade State's outreach list is not pre-sorted by carrier route, so its mail house bills at the **Mixed AADC** tier.

**Table C-1 — USPS Commercial Marketing Mail Letters, Automation Presort, per piece (3.5 oz. or less), effective July 12, 2026 [S7]**

| Presort tier | Price per piece |
|---|---|
| 5-Digit (most sorted) | $0.395 |
| 3-Digit / SCF | $0.435 |
| Mixed AADC (least sorted) | **$0.467** |

Order A mails to the full union (3,250 students): 3,250 × $0.467 = **$1,517.75**, all from Cascade State's own budget (Appendix A, MA6).

Order B mails to the intersection only (1,336 students), but this mailing is billed against CSAC's administrative allowance, not Cascade State's budget — **$0.00** to the university.

## Appendix D — What a Reminder Is Worth

No public study measures the effect of a *physical mailed* reminder on financial-aid *renewal* specifically. The closest available real evidence is a 2017 randomized controlled trial run by the U.S. General Services Administration's Office of Evaluation Sciences, in partnership with Federal Student Aid, on **emailed** FAFSA reminders to continuing students: reminder emails raised the share of targeted students who submitted a FAFSA-related action within 20 days from **4.34% to 7.77%** — a 79% relative increase — and reminders that specifically mentioned the Pell Grant were the most effective version tested [S8].

That study measured a short post-nudge window for a different channel (email, not mail) and a different action (initial submission, not full-cycle renewal), so its exact percentages cannot be dropped into this case as though they were Cascade State's own numbers. What it does establish, with real experimental evidence, is that a reminder — even a low-cost, low-effort one — produces a large, statistically real increase in on-time completion, and that Pell-specific messaging is not wasted effort. Modeling Assumptions MA4 and MA5 (Appendix A) use that direction and rough scale, not its literal figures.

## Appendix E — What a Renewal Is Worth

For context, not for the required calculations: the average Pell Grant award nationwide was **$5,300** in AY2023-24 [S2]. A first-year Cal Grant B recipient's Access Award is **$1,648** in 2025-26, with the program separately covering tuition and fees starting in the second year [S6]. A student who is eligible for both programs and misses the renewal deadline is not out a few dollars of postage — they are out thousands of dollars of aid for the following term.
