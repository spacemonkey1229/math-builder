Overview

Mia Torres has the same jar candle in her hand for the third time tonight, turning it under the garage light to check the pour line before it goes in the box. The playlist that gets her through packing nights is on shuffle. Her phone is propped against the label printer, screen still lit on the message from Rivendell Home Goods — the boutique chain that just agreed to carry Amber & Oak candles in twelve stores, so long as she can guarantee the same order every month, on time, every time. Under it, a second notification she hasn't dismissed: Amazon Seller Central, asking again whether she wants to switch her listing to Fulfilled by Amazon. She's tapped it open twice this week and closed it twice without answering.

Her tape gun is empty. She digs for a new roll in the bin under the folding table, the one already crowded with flattened boxes for tomorrow's batch. Doing it herself means nights exactly like this one, every month, for as long as the Rivendell contract runs. Handing it to Amazon means a truck picks up one big pallet and she never touches a box again — for a cut of every sale that's bigger than the one she pays now, taken before she ever sees the money.

She sets the candle down, unlit, and picks up the phone. The Seller Central screen is still open, waiting on one button.

Background

Mia Torres owns and personally runs Amber & Oak, a small-batch soy candle business she operates out of her converted garage. She is the sole owner, and she personally approves every change to how the business takes orders, ships product, and gets paid — including whether to change how her candles reach customers through her Amazon marketplace storefront, her main sales channel.

Fulfilled by Merchant (FBM)

Every unit Amber & Oak sells through Amazon currently moves through Fulfilled by Merchant — Mia herself buys the shipping label, packs the box, and hands it to the carrier, the way she has since she started the business (see Appendix A).

Fulfilled by Amazon (FBA)

Amazon offers a second path for the exact same listing: Fulfilled by Amazon — Mia ships a bulk shipment of inventory to a fulfillment center, and Amazon's own staff pick, pack, and ship each individual order to the customer.

Both paths charge Amazon's standard referral fee — a fixed percent of the item's sale price that Amazon collects on every unit sold through its marketplace, regardless of who fulfills the order (see Appendix B). FBA charges two fees on top of that: a per-unit fulfillment fee, based on the item's size and weight, that Amazon deducts for the picking, packing, and shipping work its own staff perform, and a fuel-and-logistics surcharge — a further percent that Amazon applies on top of the fulfillment fee itself, not on top of the item's price (see Appendix C). FBM has no such fees, because Mia does that work herself — but it isn't free to her, either: her own packaging materials and postage cost her a real amount on every single box she ships (see Appendix A).

The two paths also tie up Mia's money differently. Under FBM, Mia keeps only as much finished inventory on hand as she can produce and ship in small batches, which means she is carrying several weeks of finished stock, sitting on a shelf as unsold inventory, at any given moment. Under FBA, she ships one larger batch to Amazon's warehouse at a time and lets Amazon's own systems manage the pace of individual sales from there — tying up fewer weeks of finished stock as unsold inventory at once (see Appendix A). That difference in how many weeks' worth of product Mia has to keep on hand, not sold, is money she can't spend on next month's wax and wicks.

Rivendell Home Goods' new wholesale-style commitment locks in a guaranteed order quantity for this Amazon listing every single month, for as long as the contract runs (see Appendix A) — Mia has to decide which fulfillment path to run this guaranteed monthly volume through, and the decision does not reset next month. Every fee and cost figure in this case must be carried through in fraction and mixed-number form — the way Amazon's own fee schedules and Mia's own price sheet actually state them — never converted to a decimal partway through a calculation. The vocabulary and "how to…" procedures needed are listed in Appendix D. All sourced figures are cited in Appendix E.
