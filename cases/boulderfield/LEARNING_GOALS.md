## Forces test

Every mapping above passes: *could a competent student produce this deliverable correctly without
ever performing this skill?* — No, in every case:

- D1 cannot be written without dividing each round-trip mileage by 2 and without subtracting one
  route's stats from the other's to state which is shorter and which gains more.
- D2 cannot be written without substituting each route's own distance and elevation gain into
  Naismith's Rule's variable expression, carrying out the division-then-addition order of
  operations the formula requires, and rounding the result.
- D3 cannot be written without multiplying the Naismith estimate by the night-pace multiplier,
  adding The Loft's route-finding penalty, and expressing the gap between the two totals as a
  percent of the slower route's time.
- D4 cannot be written without setting up and solving an equation for an unknown rate variable,
  holding the case's other stated constants fixed.
- D5 cannot be produced without classifying and combining positive and negative numbers: the
  lapse-rate subtraction, the wind-chill formula's own multi-operation arithmetic, and the
  required subtraction of a negative wind-chill value from a positive air temperature.
- D6 cannot be produced without a real bar graph and without placing all four of D5's signed
  values (two positive, two negative) correctly on a number line.
