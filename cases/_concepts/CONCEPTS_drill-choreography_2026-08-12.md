### C1 — Company Front · score ~11.4 (raw 9.4 + 2.0 group-completion bonus — closes the entire
remaining Geometry/Transformations gap) · tier T3

**Protagonist.** Mara Okafor, assistant drill designer for her high school marching band,
finalizing a 16-count transition before the drill charts go to print.

**The dilemma.** A.) The Expand — dilate the tight block formation by a 4.4× scale factor about
its center for a full-field visual OR B.) The Mirror Turn — rotate the same block 90° about its
center, keeping every performer's travel distance small.

**Primary metric.** Option A's required step size (inches per count) as a percentage of the real
"6-to-5" aggressive step-size ceiling (30 in/step) — a threshold comparison, not a direct A-vs-B
ratio, because a tight rotation about a formation's own center is *structurally* always cheaper
than a large dilation (confirmed by direct computation — see below); comparing A's margin against
the real ceiling it might blow through is the honest shape of this dilemma, the same resolution
Tide Window used for its own non-ratio balance test.

**Why it's close.** At a 4.4× scale factor and 16 counts, Option A's farthest-out marker needs
about 27.1 in/step — over the comfortable 22.5 in/step ("8-to-5") standard but still under the
30 in/step aggressive ceiling, with under 10% headroom to spare. Option B never comes close to
either ceiling (about 11.3 in/step) by the geometry of rotating about a compact formation's own
center — real, but not a coin flip on the numbers; the coin flip is whether the visual is worth
the risk on a genuinely thin real margin.
