## C2 findings
- **Closed form reverses the window result (R1a).** At the historical geometric-mean return the 70/30 PV of the four bills is $34.5K and 30/70 is $39.4K (arithmetic mean: $32.7K vs $38.7K), so the shortcut says A is cheaper, while the window metric says B needs less at every target. The reason is dispersion and sequence risk. Either name the steady rate and teach the reversal, or stop calling series "forced."
- **Primary metric never favors A.** B needs less at 90, 95 and 100% and ties at 85%. A wins only on leftover, which is not in the metric.

## Recommended reshape for C2 (not yet reviewed)

At the historical geometric mean, steady-return PV is $34.5K (70/30) and $39.4K (30/70); the 95%-reliable simulated lump sums are $53.7K and $49.8K, so a steady-return plan underfunds A by about $19K and B by about $10K.
