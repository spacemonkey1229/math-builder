# Facilitator Notes: The Downbeat Split

**Case:** The Downbeat Split · **Tier:** T2 (MS-core) · **Studio:** Adventure
**Standards:** Fractions/Multiplication Of Fractions, Fractions/Division Of Fractions — how to
multiply two mixed numbers; how to divide a fraction by a fraction; how to multiply a fraction and
a mixed number; how to divide fractions and mixed numbers (see `case.json.standards[]` for exact
ids; new_coverage = the first two, both n=0 before this case)

Drafted at write time, before `case-research` fills Appendices A–D. Margin is `null` until the
sourced/assumed figures land — see `margin_basis` below for how it will be computed.

## Argument for A — The Straight Cut

The Straight Cut costs nothing more in rehearsal time — it is already clean, already sight-read,
already banked. The only thing it costs is a small, uniform tempo nudge across the whole cue to
absorb the leftover gap between its computed total running time (D1) and the broadcast slot's exact
target. The strongest version of this argument treats that nudge as free: the ensemble is already
warmed up on this exact material, no new rehearsal minutes are booked, and a small uniform tempo
shift is a routine adjustment engineers make on cues like this all the time. Against the primary
metric — rehearsal minutes required per second of timing accuracy gained — Option A's rehearsal-
minute numerator is at or near zero, so its ratio looks unbeatable on the page.

## Argument for B — The Downbeat Split

The Downbeat Split is the only version that lands on the broadcast slot's target exactly rather
than approximately, and a broadcast slot is not a target with a tolerance the arranger gets to set
— it is fixed by the network and the client. The strongest version of this argument is that the
Downbeat Split's rehearsal cost is small, bounded, and one-time: a fixed number of read-throughs at
the AFM's real published rehearsal rate (Appendix C), paid once, in exchange for a take that will
run on air for the life of the client's ad buy. Framed against "what does it cost to guarantee the
take actually clears delivery," rather than against a phantom free option, the extra rehearsal
minutes are cheap insurance, not overhead.

## The margin

`margin_basis`: Once Appendices A–D are sourced or their Modeling Assumptions are accepted, compute
rehearsal minutes required per second of timing accuracy gained for both options (D5's decision
table) and compare them directly. Because Option A's rehearsal-minute numerator is likely at or
near zero, the two options' ratios are not symmetric the way a typical two-variable trade-off is —
the balance instead turns on how the case frames "cost" for Option A: rehearsal minutes spent
versus risk of a take that misses the network's frame-accuracy tolerance. `margin_pct` stays `null`
until this is computed against the sourced figures.

## Five Socratic questions

1. If Option A books zero additional rehearsal minutes, what does "rehearsal minutes per second of
   timing accuracy gained" even mean for a numerator of zero — and does that make Option A
   automatically win the ratio, or does it break the ratio as a fair comparison?
2. The Straight Cut's timing gap is "close, but not exact" — how close does "close" have to be
   before the network's frame-accuracy tolerance in Appendix A treats it as a failed delivery
   instead of a rounding error?
3. D3 finds the exact subdivision needed to close the gap by dividing one fraction by another —
   what would happen to that computed subdivision if the timing gap you're closing were half as
   large, or twice as large?
4. D4 tells you how many read-throughs fit in the time remaining in the session — what happens to
   Elena's recommendation if that number comes out to zero, meaning the Downbeat Split literally
   cannot be rehearsed clean before the session ends?
5. Argument for A treats the tempo compromise as "free" because no new rehearsal time is booked —
   what real cost is that argument leaving out of the primary metric entirely?

## The trap

Heroes will price the Straight Cut's rehearsal-minute cost as zero and stop there, because the
ensemble has already learned it — comparing the Downbeat Split's real, booked rehearsal minutes
against a phantom zero instead of against the real cost the Straight Cut is hiding: the risk (and,
if it materializes, the cost) of a take that does not clear the network's frame-accuracy tolerance
and has to be re-booked entirely. "Already rehearsed" is not the same as "free" once the thing being
measured is whether the take is actually deliverable, not just whether it was cheap to record.
