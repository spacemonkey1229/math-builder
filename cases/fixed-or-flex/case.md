## Overview

Teodora Quillen stands at the back of the county board room with two printouts curling in her hand. The radiator knocks behind her, a paper cup of coffee goes cold on the windowsill, and out in the parking lot a commuter coach idles with its headlights on, fogging the glass.

The first printout is about the buses that run whether or not anyone gets on: the commuter coaches that fill before dawn with people who need to be at a desk in Pittsburgh by eight, and the local routes that carry people to the grocery store and the clinic. The second printout is about the vans that run only when somebody calls: the shared-ride trips that take a woman to dialysis, a retired teacher to the pharmacy, a man with a power wheelchair to the one job interview he has been waiting months for.

The board has found new money, once, and only one pot of it. It will go to one of those two printouts. She has spent the week building tables, and every table says something different depending on which column she reads first. Somewhere in the room a stapler clacks. Someone laughs at the wrong moment and stops.

The chair squares a folder against the table and looks up over his glasses. "Item seven," he says. "Ms. Quillen."

She peels herself off the wall and walks to the microphone with both printouts in her hand.

## Background

Teodora Quillen is a service planner at the Westmoreland County Transit Authority (WCTA), the public agency that runs buses and vans in Westmoreland County, Pennsylvania. She does not set the budget; the board does. Her job is to bring it one recommendation and the arithmetic behind it. *(Constructed framing, disclosed: the agency, its two services and every figure in Appendix A are real. Teodora, the board meeting and the new money are invented for this case. No real WCTA employee is portrayed.)*

The board has found **$100,000 a year in new, recurring operating money**. It cannot be split: it goes to one service or the other (SEE APPENDIX C). The two options, as WCTA's own website describes the services (SEE APPENDIX B):

- **More Fixed** is more *fixed-route* bus service: buses on set routes and set timetables, including the Pittsburgh commuter "Flyer" routes and the local routes. A seat on the bus exists whether or not you board.
- **More Flex** is more *demand-response* service, which WCTA brands **GO Westmoreland**: shared-ride vans that riders book ahead, picking up at the door. The Senior Shared-Ride program serves county residents aged 65 or older; the same family of services includes paratransit for riders with disabilities and medical-assistance trips.

The board's yardstick is the number of **weighted trips** the $100,000 buys in a year: trips bought at that mode's average productivity, multiplied by a **marginal-effectiveness factor** (do added hours work as hard as the average hour?) and, for vans, an **access weight** (how many fixed-route trips' worth of benefit is one van trip worth, to a rider who may have no other way to travel and who waited for the booking?). Both are labeled assumptions in Appendix C, and you will choose and defend them. (Invented example of the idea, not data from this case: if you decided one van trip is worth 1.5 fixed-route trips, then 100 van trips would score as 150 weighted trips. Nothing but the unit is borrowed from the bus.)

The newest figures on Teodora's desk are from the National Transit Database (NTD) report year 2024, with report years 2022 and 2023 for history. **Nothing later than report year 2024 is available to her** (SEE APPENDIX A and APPENDIX C, assumption M0).

The two services do not count the same kind of ride. On the fixed route, an "unlinked passenger trip" is a boarding, so a rider who transfers is counted twice. On the vans, a trip is one rider picked up and dropped off. The van service runs through programs for older adults, riders with disabilities, and medical-assistance patients; the NTD table does not say how van trips split among those programs. This case compares *service bought per dollar*. It does not rank riders, and a cost per trip says nothing about any rider's worth.

The Appendices hold the data (A), the services (B), the assumptions (C), the "How To…" list (D), plain-English notes (E) and every source (F).

## Dilemma

You are Teodora Quillen.

Do you:

**A.)** Spend the $100,000 on More Fixed, more fixed-route bus service **OR**

**B.)** Spend the $100,000 on More Flex, more GO Westmoreland demand-response service

## Appendix C — Modeling Assumptions

> These figures are **not sourced**. They are stated scenario assumptions chosen by the case author. A Hero may challenge them in discussion, and choosing your own values inside (or outside) the ranges, with a defense, is part of the work.

| # | Assumption | Starting value | Range worth arguing | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M0 | Decision date. The board holds NTD report year 2024 as the latest year; nothing later is used at the decision stage. Any later figure your facilitator shares is a review of the decision after the fact, not part of the model. | RY2024 | n/a | Freezes every figure at one date (SPEC §5). |
| M1 | The new money is $100,000 per year, recurring, and indivisible (it goes to one option). | $100,000 | n/a | Invented for the case. |
| M2 | The marginal cost of an added revenue hour equals that mode's average cost per revenue hour. Any gap between marginal and average cost is carried inside `ρ`. Cost per hour is assumed not to change between years. | average | n/a | The NTD reports no marginal-cost split. |
| M3 | `ρ_F`: how productive an added fixed-route hour is, relative to the average fixed-route hour (trips per dollar, marginal ÷ average; trips per added hour ÷ today's trips per hour is the same idea). For example, 0.90 means the next hour carries about 90% as many trips as today's average hour. | 1.00 | 0.80 – 1.05 | Added hours tend to be evenings, weekends and outer branches, which usually carry fewer riders per hour; they can also relieve crowded runs. |
| M4 | `ρ_D`: how productive an added van hour is, relative to the average van hour. | 1.00 | 0.30 – 1.10 | Extra van hours create trips only if there are riders who could not be served; the NTD reports no denied-trip or waiting-list count. |
| M5 | `w`: the access weight of one van trip, in fixed-route trips (fixed-route `w` = 1 by definition). The unit is one fixed-route trip. `w` measures how much a trip is worth *as service*: whether the rider has any other way to make it, and how long they waited for it. It is not a measure of the rider's worth, and a larger `w` does not mean a van rider matters more. A larger `w` says a van trip is worth more because the rider often has no other way to make it and may have waited for the booking. | 1.00 | 0.8 – 1.4 | A value judgment of the board, not a data point. The answer is sensitive to this value. |
| M6 | A "rider's worth" of trips: one rider making a round trip on each of 260 weekdays in a year. | 2 × 260 trips | n/a | An arithmetic convention for turning trips into people. |

The weighted-trip yardstick is: `weighted trips = (average trips per $100,000 of that mode) × ρ × w`, with `w = 1` and the fixed-route `ρ_F` for More Fixed, and `ρ_D` and `w` for More Flex.

## Appendix E — Plain-English Notes on Real-World Terms

| Term | Plain-English explanation |
|---|---|
| Fixed route | Bus service on a set path and timetable that runs whether or not a particular person boards. |
| Demand response (DR) | Van service a rider books in advance; the van is sent for that rider (and sometimes shares the ride with others). WCTA brands its version "GO Westmoreland". |
| Flyer | WCTA's name for a commuter route that runs between a Westmoreland County town and Pittsburgh. |
| Paratransit / ADA complementary paratransit | Door-to-door van service for people whose disability keeps them from using the regular bus, offered alongside bus routes under the Americans with Disabilities Act. |
| MATP | The Medical Assistance Transportation Program: rides to medical appointments for people covered by Medical Assistance (Medicaid). |
| Unlinked passenger trip | The NTD's count of boardings: each time a person gets on a vehicle counts once, so a trip with a transfer counts twice. |
| Vehicle revenue hours | One vehicle in service for one hour is one revenue hour (three buses running for an hour is three revenue hours). Hours a vehicle spends in service available to riders, not counting time driving empty to or from the garage. |
| Vehicle revenue miles | Miles vehicles travel while in service available to riders (a bus-mile or van-mile, not a rider-mile). |
| Directly operated | The agency itself, not a contractor, runs the service. |
| Deadhead | Driving a vehicle empty, such as from the garage to the start of a route; it is not counted in revenue hours. |
| Full Reporter | A larger transit agency that files the complete NTD report, which includes passenger miles; smaller agencies file less and may estimate. |
| Operating expenses | The money spent to run the service each year (drivers, fuel, maintenance, dispatch), not the price of buying buses. |
| NTD (National Transit Database) | The federal government's yearly database of what public transit agencies spend and carry. |
| Marginal vs. average | The average hour is total trips ÷ total hours. The marginal hour is the *next* hour you add, which can be more or less productive than the average. |
| Fare recovery | The share of operating expenses paid back by riders' fares. |
