## Overview

Jaylen Ortiz has the recommendation slide open and still blank on a Sunday night, laptop propped
against a stack of unopened mail, the kitchen table lit by one lamp because everyone else in the
house is asleep. Two documents sit in adjacent tabs. One is a media plan so simple it fits on a
single slide: one mention, one moment in the video, one call-to-action, running straight through
the quarter. The other is a spreadsheet Jaylen has been rebuilding all weekend — a full test
matrix, every placement timing crossed with every call-to-action style, color-coded cells
stretching past the edge of the screen.

The brand's growth lead texted twice today, both times some version of *we need real signal
before the board meeting, not a guess dressed up as a plan.* The media buyer, on a call this
afternoon, said the opposite: *every variant you add is budget you're taking away from the one
that might actually work.* Both of them are right, which is the problem.

Jaylen scrolls back to the top of the test matrix, counting the cells again, watching the number
of distinct combinations climb faster than the fixed budget the finance team has already locked
in for the quarter. Somewhere in that grid is either the most defensible test this brand has ever
run, or a plan so thinly spread that none of it will mean anything by the time the data comes
back.

The cursor blinks on the empty recommendation slide, and the coffee next to the laptop has gone
cold.

## Background

Jaylen Ortiz is the Social Media/Influencer Marketing Strategist for a mid-size direct-to-consumer
brand, responsible for recommending how the brand's paid-placement budget for its next
sponsored-video campaign should be spent, and accountable to the growth lead and finance team for
defending that recommendation before next quarter's ad budget is allocated against it (SEE
APPENDIX B). The brand pays a creator to produce one sponsored video per campaign cycle; the
question in front of Jaylen is not what the video says, but how the brand tests which
paid-placement approach around that video actually drives purchases.

Two plans are on the table:

**The Straight Cut** — a single fixed placement. The product mention airs at one fixed point in
the sponsored video, paired with one call-to-action style, for the entire campaign window (SEE
APPENDIX B).

**The Full Rotation** — a full multi-variant test. The creator cuts three separate versions of the
video, each moving the product mention to a different point — an Early Mention, a Mid Mention, and
a Late Mention — and each cut runs behind two different call-to-action styles, an Urgency CTA and a
Social-Proof CTA, for six distinct variants in total (SEE APPENDIX A).

The two plans face the same hard limits. The creator delivers only one newly edited video cut per
production week — a call-to-action style is a text overlay applied at ad-serving time and does not
require a new edit, but each placement-timing cut does — so The Full Rotation's three cuts must be
produced across whatever weeks remain in the test window before quarter-end (SEE APPENDIX A). Both
plans draw from the exact same fixed ad-spend budget the finance team has already locked in for
the quarter; The Straight Cut spends all of it behind one variant, while The Full Rotation divides
it across six (SEE APPENDIX B). And every variant, in either plan, needs to clear the ad
platform's own minimum recommended spend before its conversion numbers can be trusted as a real
read rather than noise (SEE APPENDIX C).

The Full Rotation is a genuinely bigger test — three placement timings and two call-to-action
styles is real signal a single fixed placement can never produce. But splitting one fixed budget
six ways instead of one way changes what each variant can actually prove. Every sourced and
assumed figure in this case is listed in Appendix E (Modeling Assumptions, completed once
case-research runs) and Appendix F (Sources); the vocabulary and "how to…" skills this case will
ask you to research are listed in Appendix G — that is where Challenge 1 sends you.

video cuts and both call-to-action styles
