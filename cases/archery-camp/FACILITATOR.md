# Guide Notes: Archery Camp

**Case:** Archery Camp · **Tier:** T2 · **Band:** MS · **Studio:** Heroes Academy
**Standards:** see `case.json.standards[]` for the exact ids (9 skills — decimals, multiplication,
division, combinations, equations, and comparison)

Legacy conversion, 2026-08-01, reformatted from the original 2020 Guide Notes
(`source/Y3/Additional/Archery Camp/Guide Notes.docx`) onto the current 11-anchor ladder. The
original twist (a sudden 160-registration surge) is kept, now with real, computed capacity numbers
behind it.

## Intro

Frame the format: this is a real event-planning budget with a real capacity ceiling, not a
worksheet. Name the opener — "Nine fields, two combinations, and a camp that's grown every single
year Karen has run it."

## Launch

Show both field combinations with no costs or capacities computed yet — just the letters. Ask:
"Just from the number of fields in each option, which do you think is bigger? Which do you think is
cheaper?" Do not confirm or correct answers here.

## Anchor 1 — Take a stand and vote

**TAKE A STAND AND VOTE. No reasoning yet, just commit.**

"Who rents A, B, C, G, H? Who rents D, E, F, J?" Show of hands, no discussion. Record the count —
Close will ask them to vote again.

## Anchor 2 — What is the problem we're solving?

**"Is this the right question?" · "Is it just as simple as which option is cheaper, or is there
something else to consider?"**

Let confusion grow here, deliberately. Push on Karen's own stated pattern: attendance has grown
every year she's run this camp. Is "cheapest per participant" the only real question, or does how
much room is left over matter too?

## Anchor 3 — What is the process?

**"If we go through this process and find the data, how will we decide?"**

Once the room has a working definition of the tradeoff, ask them to name the actual steps: total
each option's ranges and capacity, find the counselors needed, total the cost, divide by planned
attendance. Re-ask: "Do we agree on that process?"

## Anchor 4 — What don't we know?

**"Do we need to make assumptions? Which ones are critical?"**

Surface Appendix C's two real conventions: the 140-attendee planning figure, and that a field's
range count is fixed no matter how many campers actually show up. Ask what happens to the
comparison if either were different.

## Data Deep Dive

Now, and only now, the numbers. Walk D1-D3's capacity/counselor/cost chain together as a room,
cold-calling for the counselor-count step specifically — this is where a Hero who forgot the
"2 counselors per range, not per field" rule will get a real, visibly wrong number.

**D5, once D3 is solid.** Watch D3's total-cost step closely: the daily field rental has to be
multiplied by all 3 days of the camp, not just added once — dropping that multiplier produces a
deceptively plausible near-$110 number that still looks reasonable, and it will cascade straight
into a wrong D5 answer if it slips through uncaught. Cold-call for the ×3 step specifically before
moving on.

Archery 360's board wants $110/participant or less. Option A's fixed costs (rental + counselor pay,
both locked in once the ranges are rented, independent of how many campers actually show up) plus
its $26.30/attendee equipment rate give a real equation with the unknown attendance count on both
sides once you set it equal to $110: `12759 + 26.30x = 110x`. Cold call for the solve — and for
rounding UP, not to the nearest whole number: 152.44 people means 153 whole attendees, not 152. The
real answer, 153, is worth sitting with: **that's above Karen's own stated 150-person ceiling.**
Even Option A, the cheaper choice, cannot hit the board's target within the range Karen is actually
planning for. That's a genuine finding D6's recommendation should use, not a bolted-on algebra
drill — ask whether it changes anyone's pitch.

## Twist — drop this at ~15 minutes into the discussion

Deliver slowly, with pauses:

"A month later, Cynthia from marketing emails you: a Facebook ad went out and registration is
through the roof — more than 160 new sign-ups on top of what you planned for. Can you handle that?"

**Facilitator-only: what the numbers actually do (`case.json.derived`).** Total demand jumps to 300
campers. Option A's 156-camper max capacity comes up 144 short; Option B's 180-camper max capacity
comes up 120 short. **Neither option can actually serve everyone — but Option B turns away 24 fewer
campers than Option A.** A Hero who picked Option A on cost now has a real, harder question: is a
9.92% cheaper per-participant charge still the right call when it also means turning away more
kids? Ask: "What would you actually do next — rent more fields, cap registration, or something
else?"

Do not hand over which answer is "correct" — that's a real operational judgment call, not a math
answer the case dictates.

## Immediate recommit

Re-vote immediately. Ask by name: "Did the registration surge change your recommendation, or just
raise a new problem neither option actually solves?"

## Deep conflict — three threads (20-30 min)

1. **Cost vs. headroom.** Option A is cheaper per participant; Option B has more room to grow into.
   Is "cheapest today" the right lens for a camp that's grown every year it's existed?
2. **Whose problem is a registration surge.** Marketing wanted more sign-ups and got them — is a
   capacity shortfall a marketing win or an operations failure?
3. **What the combination count (D4) is actually for.** Karen isn't really choosing among all 126
   possible 5-field combinations — she's down to two. Does knowing the full combinatorial space
   change how confident you are that these two are the right ones to compare?

## The trap

A Hero computes the per-participant charge, finds Option A cheaper, and stops — never checking
either option's maximum capacity against Karen's own stated growth pattern. The case's real tension
is that the cheaper option today has less room for the trend Karen herself has already lived
through four years running. Watch for a pitch that reports one number and calls the case closed.

## Socratic questions

1. Option A costs $117.44 per participant and Option B costs $129.09 — a real 9.92% gap. How would you explain to Karen why that gap is or isn't worth losing 24 campers' worth of capacity over?
2. There are 126 different 5-field combinations Karen could have considered — does knowing that number make you more or less confident these particular two are the right ones to compare?
3. If Karen could only afford Option A, what would you actually recommend she do about the 160-registration surge?
4. The case fixes the planning attendance at 140 — how would the per-participant charge change if it were 100 instead? 150? (Real answer at 150: $111.36 — still above the board's $110 target.)
5. Marketing's ad worked exactly as intended — more registrations. Whose job is it to have planned for that possibility, marketing's or Karen's?

## Close

**VOTE AGAIN.** "Did we ask the right question at the start?" "What math was new to you tonight, and
how did you actually attack it?" Compare the closing vote to Anchor 1's — a room that didn't move at
all after the twist is worth asking about directly: did the capacity shortfall not feel real, or did
the room already expect growth to outpace either plan?
