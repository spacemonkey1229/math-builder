## Overview

"Please stop!" Filberto yells nothing — it's the man on the sidewalk who yells, waving both arms
at the cab like he's flagging down a plane. Filberto is only three blocks from home, radio off,
window down, when he sees him. He pulls the taxi to the curb anyway.

"Please, can you take us to the hospital?" The man's voice cracks on *hospital*. Behind him a
woman is doubled over against the hotel awning, one hand flat on her stomach, breathing in short
counted bursts. "My wife is in labor. The baby could come any minute."

Filberto has driven this city for twenty years. He has carried runaway grooms, a man with a
gunshot graze, a boy who'd never seen snow. He has never once said no to *this*. "Jes, jes, I
cansa tak yu," he says, already reaching back to pop the door.

"Thank you — thank you." Thomas half-lifts, half-guides Rachel into the back seat and climbs in
after her, pulling her hand into both of his. "It's going to be okay. We're going to make it,
honey."

Filberto checks his mirrors, pulls out onto the empty Sunday street, and glances at the woman in
the back seat — pale, sweating, counting under her breath — then at the road ahead, empty all the
way to the corner. His foot hovers over the pedal.

He is already doing exactly the speed limit.

## Background

Rachel and Thomas are in Modus City for a conference and have never been in the city before; in
the rush for the taxi, both left their phones in the hotel room. Rachel is nine months pregnant
with the couple's second child. Their first arrived just 1.5 hours after labor began — Rachel's
labor started 20 minutes ago. She is in pain and frightened, but there is no injury and no
complication beyond needing to reach the hospital before the baby does. Nobody in that back seat
wants this delivery happening in Filberto's taxi.

Filberto has driven a cab in Modus City for over twenty years and knows its streets by feel. His
last fare robbed him of his phone and cash before he picked up Rachel and Thomas — no GPS, only
the route in his head and what twenty years behind the wheel has taught him about how a car
actually moves through this city (SEE APPENDIX B):

- Stopping fully at a stop sign costs an extra 4 seconds before the car can start moving again.
- From a full stop, turning left costs 2 more seconds; turning right costs 1 more second; going
  straight through costs 1 more second.
- Cars drive on the right side of the road in Modus City, same as the USA.
- Every divided highway in Modus City has no stop signs and is posted at 45 mph.
- Every other road has a stop sign at every intersection and is posted at 25 mph.

It is early on a Sunday morning, so the streets are essentially empty — no traffic to slow him
down and, just as importantly, no traffic to hide behind.

Modus City enforces its traffic laws on taxi drivers with cameras, not officers. A single
confirmed violation — a missed stop sign, or clocking over the posted limit — is enough for the
city to permanently pull a driver's taxi license and issue a fine north of $10,000, far harsher
than an ordinary speeding ticket (SEE APPENDIX E). For Filberto, the license is not a formality.
It is the only license he has ever needed to earn a living.

Filberto's known route from the hotel to the hospital runs through four segments — two ordinary
Modus City streets, one stretch of the city's one divided highway, and one more ordinary street on
the hospital side (SEE APPENDIX A, APPENDIX C). Every segment's distance, road type, speed limit,
and stop-sign count are fixed before Filberto pulls away from the curb — what is not fixed is how
fast he drives them.

Filberto has two ways to run this route. Call the first **The Clean Run**: hold the posted limit
on every segment, camera-safe start to finish. Call the second **The Hot Run**: pick some amount
over the posted limit and hold it the whole way, trading a real but limited amount of time for a
real and severe penalty if a single camera catches him.

## Dilemma

You are Filberto.
Do you:
A.) Stay at or under the speed limit for the entire drive OR
B.) Speed to get Rachel to the hospital faster, risking Filberto's license and a $10,000+ fine

### Appendix B — Filberto's Speed & Stop-Sign Timing Reference

| Rule | Cost |
|---|---|
| Full stop at a stop sign | +4 seconds |
| Left turn from a stop | +2 seconds (on top of the stop) |
| Right turn from a stop | +1 second (on top of the stop) |
| Straight through from a stop | +1 second (on top of the stop) |
| Divided highway | no stop signs; posted 45 mph |
| Every other road | stop sign at every intersection; posted 25 mph |

Worked example: one stop sign followed by a left turn costs 4 + 2 = 6 seconds total before the
car is back up to speed.
