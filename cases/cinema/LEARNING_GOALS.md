## The forces test

*Could a competent student produce this deliverable correctly without ever performing this skill?*

- **D1** — cannot build a real showtime-by-showtime schedule without adding a movie's runtime to
  its start time (add/subtract time), checking a showtime lands within the required 60/90-minute
  cadence window (elapsed time), converting the 5-seconds-per-seat cleaning formula from seconds
  to minutes (convert units of time) to compare it against the schedule, and multiplying seconds
  per seat by seat count to get total cleaning time (multiply and divide time). No route around
  any of these.
- **D2** — cannot honestly state "movie X ran N times Thursday" without comparing that count
  against the movie's own contracted minimum (compare two or more values) to check the schedule
  is legal.
- **D3** — cannot compute a single slot's ticket revenue without applying a capacity percent to a
  seat count (percent of a number / percent), multiplying seats x capacity x price (multiply
  decimals), and cannot honestly compare two dollar totals without rounding both to the same
  place (round to a specified place value / rounding) — which requires identifying the hundredths
  (cents) place (place value) in the first place.
- **D4** — cannot turn Appendix B's five-way concession-spending breakdown into one number per
  ticket without computing a weighted average (mean of a set of data / mean), then adding that
  figure to D3's ticket revenue (add decimals).
- **D5** — cannot reach a net-profit figure without subtracting cost from revenue (subtract
  decimals / subtraction), cannot total either plan's full weekend figure without summing across
  four days (addition), and cannot state a recommendation without comparing the two final totals
  (compare two or more values).
