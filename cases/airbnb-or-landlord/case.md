## Overview

"Congratulations, you two — we're scheduled to close in thirty days!" The realtor's voice crackles through the phone, then goes quiet. Wendy Dalton lowers the phone from her ear and looks across the kitchen at Dwayne, who already has a roll of packing tape in one hand and a half-flattened box in the other.

Thirty days. That's a paint touch-up, a dumpster rental, six years of school photos to sort through — and, somewhere in there, a decision about the house they're about to leave behind.

Dwayne sets the tape down and turns his laptop around so Wendy can see it. Two browser tabs sit open side by side. One is a host dashboard, all red and green calendar squares and a nightly rate ticking in the corner. The other is a single plain number, repeated twelve times down a spreadsheet column.

"So which one are we actually doing?" Wendy asks, leaning over his shoulder. Down the hall, one of the kids drags a moving box across the hardwood like a sled, and nobody tells them to stop.

Dwayne's cursor hovers between the two tabs, waiting for one of them to make the choice obvious.

It doesn't.

## Background

Dwayne and Wendy Dalton bought their current home years ago and have kept it up ever since — new appliances, a new water heater, a new roof. It's fully remodeled and furnished, eight minutes from the airport, and close to shopping, restaurants, and a movie theater. The backyard has a covered patio and a swing set, and the yard is serviced every week. Now that the family is moving into a bigger home nearby, the Daltons have already decided the old house becomes an income property — they are not selling it. They intend to rent it out for at least the next ten years. What they haven't decided is *how* (SEE APPENDIX A for the home's full specs and costs).

**Option 1 — Airbnb.** Boise's short-term rental market is real but still developing compared to larger metro areas (SEE APPENDIX B). Renting nightly means no guest is ever guaranteed — some nights sit empty. Cleaning happens after every single stay, and the Daltons plan to do it themselves: about two hours per turnover, with real cleaning materials costing **$25 per cleaning**. They plan to charge guests a separate **$65 cleaning fee** on top of the nightly rate for each stay, which is standard Airbnb practice.

**Option 2 — Traditional rental.** Boise's long-term rental market is in high demand, with new apartment construction going up across the metro and rental homes increasingly scarce (SEE APPENDIX C). A traditional rental means signing one renter to a 1- or 2-year lease: no nightly vacancy risk, and — because the home comes fully furnished — the Daltons can charge a real market premium over an unfurnished comparable (SEE APPENDIX D).

Whichever option the Daltons choose, the house itself costs the same to hold either way: a **$1,092 monthly mortgage payment** and **$490 per year in additional maintenance** regardless of who's renting it or how (SEE APPENDIX A). Utilities are the one cost that splits by option — Airbnb guests expect utilities included in the nightly rate, industry-standard for short-term rentals, while a traditional 12-month tenant pays their own utility bills, which is the standard convention for a residential lease.

The move to the new house isn't free, either. Starting next month, the Daltons take on a **new second mortgage payment of $2,300 per month** on top of everything else. Dwayne and Wendy both work full-time locally and together bring home **$8,200 per month**, against **$4,000 per month** in other household expenses. Whatever this rental property earns has to fit into that picture (SEE APPENDIX E). Every dollar figure in this case that isn't a direct citation is a disclosed, stated Modeling Assumption (SEE APPENDIX F) — none is invented quietly, and every cited figure is sourced (SEE APPENDIX G).

The vocabulary and "how to…" skills this case will ask you to research are listed in Appendix H — that is where Challenge 1 sends you.

### Appendix D — Furnished-Rental Premium and the Airbnb Host Fee

**Furnished-rental premium.** Landlords can typically charge a real, market-documented **15% to 30% more per month** for a furnished long-term rental compared to an identical unfurnished unit — the exact number depends on furniture quality, bundled amenities, and local demand. Source: furnished-rental industry guide, accessed 2026-08-20 [S3].

**Airbnb host service fee.** Airbnb runs two fee structures side by side as it migrates hosts through 2026: an older **split-fee** model (~3% from the host, a separate ~14–16.5% from the guest) and the newer **single host-only fee** (**15.5%**, most hosts, deducted entirely from the host's payout). This case models the Daltons under the newer single host-only structure, since that is the one most hosts are on by the time this case is set — 15.5%, applied to the nightly rate *and* any fees the host charges (including the cleaning fee), but not to taxes. Source: Airbnb Help Center, "Airbnb service fees," accessed 2026-08-20 [S4].
