Overview

"Time for family council!" Rachel yells out the door of the Davidson's RV. Her four kids come running from the grove of trees, little Henry's shorts wet, of course, after playing in the creek. The Davidsons have been living happily in an RV on their property for months as they design and build their dream house.

The family gathers around the fire pit. Rachel nods at her husband Cooper, acknowledging they should begin. "Alright kids, your mom and I have narrowed our house plans down to two, and we need your help picking which one we should build."

Cooper spreads two rolled floor plans across the picnic table, weighing them down with river stones so the evening breeze doesn't send them into the fire. "This one," he says, tapping the smaller roll, "or this one," tapping the larger.

Rachel looks at the loan paperwork sitting under her coffee mug, then back at the kids' faces around the fire. "Whichever one we pick has to actually work — for the house we want, and for the numbers the bank gave us."

Background

The Davidsons sold their starter home earlier this year and, with Cooper now able to work from home, purchased a wooded property for their long-term dream home. They already own the lot, so their only costs are the house build itself and any additional amenities (SEE APPENDIX C).

Two floor plans have been finalized: the Country Farmhouse (SEE APPENDIX A) and the larger Ranch Estate (SEE APPENDIX B). Only heated, covered living space counts toward a plan's square footage — porches, garages, and attics do not.

Each floor plan can be built at one of three finish tiers — Standard, Plus, or Luxury — priced per square foot of heated space (SEE APPENDIX D). The family's lender has approved a $495,000 loan, on the condition that the loan covers no more than 90% of the total project cost (build plus any amenities); the Davidsons would cover the remaining 10% themselves. That condition sets the real ceiling on what they can spend — not the loan number alone (SEE APPENDIX C).

The Davidsons want the finish quality and extra amenities that make a house feel like their dream home — but not at the cost of going over what the lender, and their own savings, can actually support.
