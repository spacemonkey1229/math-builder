**Why each mapping holds:**

- **D1** cannot be produced without the exact procedure the spine names: given a set's mean and
  all-but-one of its elements, solve algebraically for the missing element (`mean × n − known
  sum`). A student who guesses a plausible-looking number instead of running this algebra has not
  produced the deliverable — the whole point of D1 is that the reconstructed value is *derived*,
  not estimated. It also requires knowing what a mean is well enough to invert the formula.
- **D2** requires computing the mean of two different-sized datasets (52-member reconstructed vs.
  51-member dropped) and the quartiles each box-and-whisker plot needs — a student who has never
  learned to find Q1/Q3 for an ordered dataset cannot produce the table this deliverable asks for.
- **D3** is, by definition, the box-and-whisker-plot skill applied twice to two different datasets
  built in D2. There is no other way to produce two correctly-labeled box plots.
- **D4** requires recognizing whether the circle's most-connected known member (degree 34, the
  largest value in Appendix B) still reads as a statistical outlier once quartiles shift under
  each option — a student who does not know what an outlier is, relative to the whiskers computed
  in D3, cannot answer this question correctly, and it also requires recomputing and comparing
  each option's mean, tying back to the mean skill.
- **D5** cannot correctly open by "citing your D1 reconstructed value" without D1 having actually
  used the missing-data-from-mean procedure — a memo that just states a number without showing the
  algebra has not used the deliverable it claims to cite.
