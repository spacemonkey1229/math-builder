## Overview

The wall-sized video board in the National Interagency Fire Center's command operations room is a wall of red. Satellite thermal overlays crawl across it, on top of a live news feed showing a hillside glowing orange against a pre-dawn sky. Response Commander Rex Dobson hasn't sat down in six hours.

"Sir, the governor is ready to deploy the National Guard — he's on line two." A voice cuts across the room. Dobson doesn't look up from the map table, where two grease-pencil arrows are still drying: one pointed at San Fernando, one at Santa Clarita.

Overnight reports just confirmed the fire tore through the town of Agua Dulce faster than anyone on shift expected. The smokejumper teams radioed in fresh spread numbers off both flanks twenty minutes ago. Somewhere in this room is the answer to where the rapid response force should go — and Dobson has line two waiting.

"I'll take it in my office," he says, and starts walking.

---

## Background

Fueled by record-high temperatures and a dry winter, it has been a brutal fire season across California and the wider western United States.

The Soledad Canyon Fire began three days ago from an unknown cause. Initially 55% contained, it exploded in size, speed, and intensity yesterday when Santa Ana winds off the northwest Sierra Pelona combined with high heat and low humidity. Local authorities in Santa Clarita, San Fernando, and the smaller mountain communities between them, working with Cal Fire, have ordered evacuations across the fire's path (**SEE APPENDIX B AND APPENDIX C**).

The California National Guard is preparing a force of 2,500 troops, but only a **rapid response force of 235 troops** is ready to deploy today, and it can only go to one place. Its job on arrival is to cut a fire line along the edge of an urban area, to keep the fire from reaching homes and businesses there.

At 05:00 this morning, two field teams radioed in independent spread reports (**SEE APPENDIX D** for how these numbers are used):

- A smokejumper team on the fire's **southern head** reported a Rate of Spread (ROS) of **35 chains per hour**, on a 9% slope, with a 10 mph wind.
- A firefighter team on the fire's **southwestern flank**, on the highway just east of Solemint, reported an ROS of **27 chains per hour**, on a 4% slope, with an 8 mph wind.

The National Weather Service's forecast for today (**SEE APPENDIX A**) shows temperature, wind, and humidity all shifting through the day — the two 05:00 field reports are the most current numbers Commander Dobson has, but they are not the only numbers on the table.

Real population and structure data for the two candidate towns, and for Agua Dulce (already hit), are in **APPENDIX C**. Real distances from the fire's current position near Agua Dulce to each town are in **APPENDIX C** as well. Every figure in Appendices A through D is cited in **APPENDIX E**, and further fire/terrain reference material for your pitch is in **APPENDIX F**. The vocabulary and "how to…" skills this case draws on are listed in **Appendix G** — that is where Challenge 1 sends you.

## Appendix B — Fire Perimeter and Population Context

The Soledad Canyon Fire sits in the mountains and canyons between the town of Agua Dulce (already struck, per Background) and two much larger population centers: San Fernando to the south and Santa Clarita to the southwest. The fire's current position is modeled at Agua Dulce for the distance calculations in Appendix C (Modeling Assumption MA3) — Agua Dulce is the last confirmed location the fire reached.

The terrain between the fire and both towns is a mix of canyon, ridge, and rural/exurban development, with Soledad Canyon Road and San Fernando Road forming the main corridors the fire and any evacuation traffic would follow. [S6]

## Appendix D — Modeling Assumptions

> These figures are **not** independently sourced predictions of how this fire will actually behave — they are stated scenario conventions the case fixes so every Hero's numbers can be compared. A Hero may challenge any of them in discussion; that is a legitimate move, and Appendix A is deliberately left in the packet to make that challenge possible.

| # | Assumption | Value/rule used | Basis | Sensitivity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MA1 | D1's acreage is modeled as a **sector**, not a full circle, grown from a point at the higher reported rate (35 ch/hr, southern head) over the hours from 05:00 to 23:59, using the real 45° compass angle between due-south (the head) and due-southwest (the flank). | Sector angle = 45°; rate = 35 ch/hr; elapsed time = 18 h 59 min | A full-circle model overstates the area by 8×; the fire's own two reported bearings bound a real 45° wedge between the head and the flank. | Anticipated acreage scales with the square of elapsed time and directly with the sector angle — a 4-hour earlier check-in or a narrower confirmed wedge both cut the estimate sharply. |
| MA2 | D2 applies Agua Dulce's real housing density (Appendix C) to D1's acreage, with the same occupancy rate and household size, to estimate homes/people newly at risk tonight. | 0.0891 housing units/acre; 95.0% occupied; 2.79 persons/household | Agua Dulce is the closest real place with published density data sitting in the same terrain the fire is currently burning through. | If the true density of the newly-threatened ground is lower (more of it is undeveloped wildland), this is a real overestimate — flag this explicitly as an upper bound, not a forecast. |
| MA3 | The fire's current position, for distance purposes, is modeled at Agua Dulce. | — | Agua Dulce is the last confirmed location reached (Background). | If the fire's actual leading edge is already several miles past Agua Dulce toward one town, that town's time-to-arrival in D3/D4 shrinks accordingly. |
| MA4 | Real driving-route mileage (Appendix C) is used as the distance the fire would have to cover to reach each town, rather than straight-line distance. | 19.3 mi / 25.3 mi | Santa Ana wind-driven brush fires in this terrain tend to run along canyon and ridge corridors that roughly parallel the road network, not in a straight line over the mountains. | A straight-line (shorter) distance would reduce both D3 and D4's times, but likely by a similar proportion for each — the recommendation is sensitive to this modeling choice, so check whether it survives switching to straight-line distance. |
| MA5 | Each flank's 05:00 ROS is held constant for the full period modeled in D1–D4. | — | This is the most current field data Commander Dobson has. | Appendix A's forecast shows wind climbing sharply after 11:00 — a Hero who accounts for that should expect both D1's acreage and D3/D4's arrival times to be **overestimates of the time available**, i.e., the real danger arrives sooner than this model says. |
