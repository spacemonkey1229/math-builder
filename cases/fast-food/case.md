## Overview

"Are we really going to do this?" Devin asks, pulling into the garage with dinner from their favorite drive-thru still warm on the passenger seat. "Yeah," Chris says, killing the engine but not moving to get out. "We've talked about it for months. The timing's right — the house sale closed, the neighborhood's exploding, and we've got people who actually want to invest with us."

Devin exhales, feeling the mix of excitement and nausea that comes with betting a chunk of your savings on a business you don't own yet. She pulls a folder off the dash — two franchise packets, both dog-eared from weeks of research, both promising, both real.

"I'll get everyone together next week," she says. "But we walk in with an actual pick. Not 'we're leaning toward' — a pick, with numbers."

Chris nods slowly, already running the problem in his head the way he runs every problem at work. Two logos. Two very different bets. One presentation, five days away, in front of the people trusting them with real money.

He reaches for his laptop.

## Background

Devin Reynolds (35) is a primary care physician at St. Luke's Hospital in Meridian, Idaho. Her husband Chris (34) is a mechanical engineer in Boise who is ready to leave his job to run the franchise full-time if the group moves forward — which means the business needs a real staff on day one, not just an owner-operator.

After selling a home Devin owned before their marriage, the couple has **$186,000** in liquid savings plus **$345,000** in home-sale proceeds. A small group of family and friends has offered to invest alongside them, adding **$275,000** in committed capital (SEE APPENDIX A for how each franchise's own start-up cost compares against the group's combined total).

Chris and Devin first looked at their own favorite chains, but the existing franchisees in the Meridian area hold territorial rights that block any new location for those brands. After months of research, two other national franchise opportunities remain live options: a Subway franchise and a Papa John's franchise (SEE APPENDIX A AND APPENDIX B). The group wants to build in Meridian, Idaho, a fast-growing suburb of Boise (SEE APPENDIX D for the real growth data behind that pitch).

Whichever franchise they choose, the investor group meets in five days to hear a specific financial plan: the total start-up investment and staffing needed to open, the franchise's ongoing fees and first-year operating costs, projected first-year revenue, and how long it will take the business to pay back what everyone put in (SEE APPENDIX C for the modeling assumptions behind the staffing and operating-cost figures, APPENDIX E for the full source citations behind every figure, and APPENDIX F for the "how to…" math questions the case draws on).

## Dilemma

You are Chris and Devin Reynolds.

Do you:

**A.)** Pitch to purchase a Subway franchise **OR**

**B.)** Pitch to purchase a Papa John's franchise

## Challenges

1.) Research and find an online resource that answers one of the "how to…" questions from the case study launch. Post a link to the resource as your submission.

2.) Post 250 or more "excellent" words pitching a firm stance to your audience on which you would choose and why. Your pitch must include:
    a.) Use of the "how to…" resource you researched in Challenge 1.
    b.) At least one original visual display of the data.

3.) Include the following:

- **D1 — A start-up cost check**: add up the group's three funding sources from Background to find their total available capital, then report your chosen franchise's franchise fee and total initial investment range from Appendix A — alongside the number of employees you'll need to hire by Day 1 — and determine whether the top of that investment range fits under the group's capital, and by how much.
- **D2 — A first-year operating cost table** for your chosen franchise: royalty fee, ad/marketing fund contribution, food cost, and labor cost, each computed as a percent of your D3 revenue figure, from Appendix B's published/benchmark rates.
- **D3 — Projected first-year revenue and net profit** for your chosen franchise: revenue from Appendix B's average unit volume (AUV) figure, minus your D2 percent-of-revenue costs and Appendix C's monthly fixed operating costs (annualized).
- **D4 — Break-even point in months**: set up and solve a one-step equation — the midpoint of your D1 investment range equals your average monthly net profit (from D3) times the number of months — for the number of months.
- **D5 — Break-even monthly revenue**: set up and solve an equation with the unknown revenue amount on both sides — monthly revenue equals Appendix C's monthly fixed cost plus your D2 percent-of-revenue rate applied to that same unknown revenue — then compare it to your actual projected monthly revenue from D3.
- **D6 — A recommendation** comparing both franchises' first-year return on investment (D3's net profit ÷ D1's investment midpoint, as a percent), informed by D4 and D5.

4.) An appendix showing evidence of your original thinking, work, calculations and processes you used to complete the challenges (pictures, screenshots, notes, etc.). Your work in the appendix does not count toward your word count.

5.) Participate twice or more in the Case Study discussion. *If you are absent the day of a discussion post 500 "excellent" words on the questions above.
