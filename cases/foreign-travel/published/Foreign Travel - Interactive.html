Overview

“Good morning, sis,” Natalie calls out, shuffling toward the kitchen to put the kettle on. Katie is already at the table, a laptop propped open between a cold bowl of cereal milk and a fan of printed maps.

“Some research?” Natalie says, eyeing the spread. “Looks like you’re back in grad school.”

“The trip is only a few weeks away,” Katie says, not looking up. “It’s not going to plan itself. I don’t want to waste our first afternoon in London figuring out which train to catch.”

“Train? We’re renting a car. It’s easier — we go wherever we want, whenever we want.”

Katie snorts. “You, driving in Europe? Different signs, different languages, the wrong side of the road? Come on. Rail is more fun. We can walk around, get snacks, watch the country go by. It’ll be like our favorite movie — Murder on the Orient Express, minus the murder.”

“I have to get ready for work.” Natalie grabs her coat off the chair. “Tonight — can we settle this tonight? I’ll grab pizza from Antonio’s on the way home.”

“Pineapple,” Katie calls after her. “You know it’s the best.”

The door clicks shut behind Natalie. Katie looks down at the two folders sitting side by side on the table — one labeled RAIL, one labeled CAR — and starts making notes for tonight.

Background

Sisters Natalie and Katie Majors have been planning a European trip together for years, and this is finally the year it happens. Flights are booked. AirBnBs are reserved. The only thing left to decide is how they’ll actually get around once they land — and the sisters don’t agree.

Their flight lands at London Gatwick Airport on a Friday morning, and their return flight departs from the same airport nine days later, in the evening. In between, their AirBnb bookings lock in a nine-day itinerary: London (2 nights) → Dover (1 night, via Warner Bros. Studio Tour and Stonehenge) → Paris (2 nights) → Amsterdam (2 nights, with a Groningen day trip) → Canterbury (1 night, via Brussels) → airport.

Both sisters agree on one thing without argument: inside the cities of London and Paris, they’ll get around by metro/subway, not by car (see Appendix C). What they don’t agree on is everything between the cities.

Natalie’s plan — Option A, the Rental Car. Pick up a rental car the moment they land at Gatwick, drive themselves the entire route, and drop the car back at the same Gatwick counter on the last day (see Appendix A). It means crossing the English Channel by car (see Appendix D) and figuring out what to do with the car while it sits idle during the London and Paris city days, including any fee a city charges just for driving a car in (see Appendix C).

Katie’s plan — Option B, Rail. Book every intercity leg on a train — Eurostar under the Channel to Paris, on to Amsterdam, up to Brussels and back to England — reading timetables and connections in advance (see Appendix B).

The sisters are traveling light, so luggage space isn’t a constraint. What is a real constraint: they are splitting every transportation cost of the trip evenly, and whichever plan wins tonight’s argument is the plan for the whole nine days — no switching partway through. Curious about the real places on this itinerary — the Channel Tunnel, Eurostar, Stonehenge, the Studio Tour, Groningen — see Appendix G for further reading.

Appendix E — Currency & Calendar Modeling Assumptions

    Twist

    Ask your facilitator before revealing this — in a live discussion, the Twist is dropped at a specific moment, not read in advance.

    Reveal the Twist

    The question: somewhere in the middle of this trip, both the UK and mainland Europe change their clocks for daylight saving — on the exact same day (Appendix E). Does that mean the London–Europe time offset used throughout Appendix B needs to be computed differently for legs before that Sunday versus legs after it?

    The honest, sourced answer: no. Because the UK and the EU both spring forward on the same calendar date, the one-hour gap between London time and mainland-Europe time is exactly the same on both sides of that Sunday. Nothing about the cross-Channel offset actually changes over the course of this trip — even though something in the world visibly did.
