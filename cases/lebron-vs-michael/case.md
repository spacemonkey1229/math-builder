## Overview

Betsy knocks on Robert's office door. "You wanted to see me, sir?" "Yes," replies Robert. "We're assigning next month's cover story to you." "Thank you, sir!" an excited Betsy responds. "With the NBA awards coming up, we want you to do a piece on the NBA's GOAT debate — LeBron vs. Michael."

"That's a doozy," says Betsy, taking a deep breath. "Do you have a thought on who we should choose?" she asks. "You're the magazine's NBA expert — I'm confident you'll figure it out," responds Robert. As Betsy stands to leave, Robert winks and teases: "We both know it's Jordan though, right?"

Betsy sits back down at her desk, pulls up two open browser tabs, and stares at them for a long moment. Two of the greatest careers the sport has ever seen, and her editor already thinks he knows the answer. She's going to need more than a gut feeling to change his mind — or confirm it.

## Background

Betsy Naismith, a descendant of James Naismith, the inventor of basketball, started as a beat writer for the Dallas Mavericks nine years ago and has won several sports journalism awards. Three years ago, she was promoted to lead journalist at Sports Athletic Magazine, where she covers the NBA in depth.

Betsy grew up in Ohio in the 1990s and fell in love with the sport watching Michael Jordan and the Chicago Bulls win championship after championship. Her entire professional career has taken place during the rise of LeBron James, who has led three different franchises to the NBA Finals.

Both players' full regular-season and playoff statistics are in **APPENDIX A** and **APPENDIX B**. Both players' real career seasons and accolades are in **APPENDIX C** and **APPENDIX D**. ## Appendix E: Modeling Assumptions

**Assumption 1 — Career percentages are makes over attempts, career-wide.** A career 2-point/3-point/free-throw percentage is total career makes divided by total career attempts (Appendix A/B) — not the average of each season's own percentage. Averaging season percentages directly would let a handful of small-sample seasons count exactly as much as a full 82-game season, which distorts the real career number.

**Assumption 2 — Career means are weighted by games played.** Career mean assists/rebounds per game is total career assists/rebounds divided by total career games played (Appendix A/B) — the same reasoning as Assumption 1, applied to counting stats instead of percentages.

**Assumption 3 — Career year, not calendar season.** Appendix C indexes each season by "Career Year" (Year 1 = each player's rookie season) rather than the actual calendar season, so that Jordan's real mid-career retirement and both players' real career lengths don't misalign the comparison.

**Assumption 4 — Playoff totals are reconstructed from per-game data.** The original source only records real playoff stats as a per-game average for each season, not a season total. Appendix B's playoff totals are each season's real per-game average multiplied by that season's real games played, summed across all seasons, and rounded to the nearest whole make/attempt/assist/rebound — a close estimate of the true career playoff total, not the exact box-score sum.

**Assumption 5 — A fixed real cutoff date, not "current" stats.** LeBron James is still an active player, so any real career statistic of his is a moving target. This case freezes every one of his numbers (Appendix A, B, C, D) at the real end of his 18th season, the 2020-21 season — not at whatever his stats happen to be on the day you read this. Jordan's career is complete, so his numbers are simply his real final career totals throughout.
