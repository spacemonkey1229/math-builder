Overview

Betsy knocks on Robert's office door. "You wanted to see me, sir?" "Yes," replies Robert. "We're assigning next month's cover story to you." "Thank you, sir!" an excited Betsy responds. "With the NBA awards coming up, we want you to do a piece on the NBA's GOAT debate — LeBron vs. Michael."

"That's a doozy," says Betsy, taking a deep breath. "Do you have a thought on who we should choose?" she asks. "You're the magazine's NBA expert — I'm confident you'll figure it out," responds Robert. As Betsy stands to leave, Robert winks and teases: "We both know it's Jordan though, right?"

Betsy sits back down at her desk, pulls up two open browser tabs, and stares at them for a long moment. Two of the greatest careers the sport has ever seen, and her editor already thinks he knows the answer. She's going to need more than a gut feeling to change his mind — or confirm it.

Background

Betsy Naismith, a descendant of James Naismith, the inventor of basketball, started as a beat writer for the Dallas Mavericks nine years ago and has won several sports journalism awards. Three years ago, she was promoted to lead journalist at Sports Athletic Magazine, where she covers the NBA in depth.

Betsy grew up in Ohio in the 1990s and fell in love with the sport watching Michael Jordan and the Chicago Bulls win championship after championship. Her entire professional career has taken place during the rise of LeBron James, who has led three different franchises to the NBA Finals.

Both players' full regular-season and playoff statistics are in APPENDIX A and APPENDIX B. Both players' real career seasons and accolades are in APPENDIX C and APPENDIX D. Exactly how career averages are calculated — and how to compare seasons of different lengths fairly — is fixed in APPENDIX E; use those conventions, or your numbers won't match anyone else's.

Every one of Betsy's numbers comes out of a box score, and every argument she can make hinges on the difference between a regular season and the playoffs, or between a field goal percentage and a free throw percentage. Before she can even open a spreadsheet, she has to be fluent in the vocabulary the league itself uses to decide things like a scoring title or a Finals MVP.

The vocabulary and the "how to…" skills this case will ask of you are listed in Appendix G — that is where Challenge 1 sends you. Want to read more about the two players and the debate itself, beyond the required math? See Appendix F.

Appendix E — Modeling Assumptions

Assumption 1 — Career percentages are makes over attempts, career-wide. A career 2-point/3-point/free-throw percentage is total career makes divided by total career attempts (Appendix A/B) — not the average of each season's own percentage.

Assumption 2 — Career means are weighted by games played. Career mean assists/rebounds per game is total career assists/rebounds divided by total career games played (Appendix A/B).

Assumption 3 — Career year, not calendar season. Appendix C indexes each season by "Career Year" (Year 1 = each player's rookie season) rather than the actual calendar season.

Assumption 4 — Playoff totals are reconstructed from per-game data. The source only records real playoff stats as a per-game average for each season. Appendix B's playoff totals are each season's real per-game average multiplied by that season's real games played, summed, and rounded to the nearest whole make/attempt/assist/rebound/block — a close estimate, not the exact box-score sum. LeBron's playoff Blocks total (253) carries the widest estimate margin of any figure in Appendix B; Jordan's (158) is a directly reported career sum, not a reconstruction.

Assumption 5 — The missing-data exercise uses a simple mean, not the weighted career mean. D2's missing-season exercise uses the simple arithmetic mean of LeBron's 18 real season assist totals (sum ÷ 18 = 538.67), not the games-weighted career mean used elsewhere in D2 (7.40 assists/game) — two different, both-real numbers answering two different questions.

Assumption 6 — A fixed real cutoff date, not "current" stats. LeBron James is still an active player, so any real career statistic of his is a moving target. This case freezes every one of his numbers (Appendix A, B, C, D) at the real end of his 18th season, the 2020-21 season — not at whatever his stats happen to be on the day you read this. Jordan's career is complete, so his numbers are simply his real final career totals throughout.

    Working Notebook

    This is a calculator, not an answer key. Type your own numbers first — then hit Check to see if your arithmetic matches. It won't tell you which player to choose; that part is still yours.

    Step 1 — Career Regular-Season Shooting

    Using Appendix A's real career totals and Assumption 1 (makes ÷ attempts, career-wide), find each player's career 2-point, 3-point, and free-throw percentage.

    Step 2 — Career Regular-Season Means

    Using Appendix A and Assumption 2 (total ÷ games played, career-wide), find each player's mean assists per game and mean rebounds per game.

    Step 2b — Missing Data Element, Given the Mean

    LeBron's 18 real career-year assist totals (Appendix C) have a simple mean of 538.67 (sum ÷ 18 — Assumption 5, not the games-weighted rate from Step 2). If you only knew 17 of the 18 totals and this mean, you could recover the 18th: missing = (mean × 18) − (sum of the other 17). Try it for Career Year 18, then check against the real value already in Appendix C.
