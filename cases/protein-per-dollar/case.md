## Overview

The walk-in cooler hums at 6:40 a.m., and Esperanza Haldane is sliding the last flats of eggs off a shelf that was full on Monday. The cold bites her wrists. Behind her a volunteer reads the sign-in sheet aloud, and the list is longer than last month's again. Not by much. She has stopped being surprised.

Last week a client stopped her at the loading door. Her kids will eat protein that comes out of the fridge, she said, not another bag of beans. The regional food bank's cold-chain agreement for this grant line says the same thing, in contract language.

Esperanza is the pantry's purchasing coordinator, and today she places the standing order for the whole quarter. Two supplier quotes are printed and clipped to the cooler door: a pallet of eggs, and cases of chicken breast. The egg quote came in higher than the last one. After the 9:00 a.m. cutoff the order locks. She remembers last winter, when a pantry across town guessed wrong and ran short of protein before the quarter was out. Nobody there had been careless. They had trusted the wrong week.

Beside the door a small boy stacks egg cartons into a tower, slowly, his tongue between his teeth. It leans. The compressor kicks on. Esperanza unclips both quotes and holds them side by side under the buzzing light, one in each hand.

## Background

Esperanza Haldane is the purchasing coordinator for a mid-size community food pantry. (Esperanza and the pantry are invented for this case; the prices, the nutrition sources and the funding rule are not.) On a Friday in mid-September 2024 she places the pantry's standing order (the same purchase, repeated every quarter) of refrigerated fresh protein for the coming quarter, October through December. She chooses the protein, and she answers to the pantry's board for how much protein the grant money buys.

The two supplier quotes in the story (a pallet of eggs, cases of chicken breast) are not reproduced here; the case uses the BLS average prices described below in their place, and works in dozens and pounds. The two options are **Eggs** (Grade A large eggs, bought by the dozen) and **Chicken** (fresh boneless chicken breast, bought by the pound).

**The money and the order.** The grant line (a pot of grant money set aside for one purpose) for refrigerated fresh protein is $12,000 [MA1] a month, $36,000 for the quarter (SEE APPENDIX A). The supplier's contract lets the pantry place one protein per quarterly order, so the choice is one or the other. The order locks the protein and the monthly spend, and each month the supplier ships as many units as that spend buys at that month's going price. This case uses the national average price for the month, published by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS), as the stand-in for the supplier's price [S4]; it is an average of retail prices, not a quote from any one supplier or town (SEE APPENDICES A AND D).

**The metric.** The board scores the order by grams of protein per dollar. Esperanza plans with the latest month of price data published on the day she orders: August 2024 (SEE APPENDIX A). The monthly price history back to January 2006 is in (SEE APPENDIX B). The conversion factors are in (SEE APPENDIX C). The nutrition values are stated assumptions, listed with their basis in (SEE APPENDIX D). No price after August 2024 appears anywhere in this packet.

**Why dry beans are not on the ballot.** Dry beans cost less per gram of protein, and their price is in (SEE APPENDIX A). They are not an option for this order because of a funding rule: this grant line is paid under the regional food bank's cold-chain agreement, which limits it to refrigerated protein, and shelf-stable protein (food that keeps without refrigeration) is bought from a separate pantry-staple fund that is already spent for the quarter. The rule is about which money may be spent on what. It is not a judgment about what families should eat, and the pantry's clients have also asked for fresh protein. Your pitch must say what the rule costs (Deliverable D6).

All figures in this case are sourced or declared in (SEE APPENDICES D AND E). The math skills and vocabulary this case asks you to research are listed in (SEE APPENDIX F).

## Dilemma

You are Esperanza Haldane.

Do you:

**A.)** Spend the quarter's refrigerated-protein budget on Grade A large eggs **OR**

**B.)** Spend it on fresh boneless chicken breast

Then a paragraph on what grams per dollar leaves out (for example fat, how much of what you buy is edible, how families cook and accept the food, cold storage, and the risk that the price moves during the quarter) and what the pantry gives up under the funding rule.
