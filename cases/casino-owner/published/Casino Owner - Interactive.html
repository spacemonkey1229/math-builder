Overview

The dealer's chips click against each other in a steady rhythm, a sound Emilia Carlon has learned to hear underneath everything else on the floor — the slot bells, the low roar of a table that just hit big, a cocktail tray clinking past on someone's shoulder. She's been walking this floor for two years and she still checks the pit count out of habit, out of the corner of her eye, without breaking stride.

The elevator chimes. She doesn't need to turn around to know who it is — the two security guards step out first, then the assistant, then Danny Paseronopolis, exactly on schedule, the way he always is.

“How is everything this evening?”

“Running smoothly. Tables are full, should be a good night.”

“Very good.” He says it the way he always says it when he's pleased, which is not often enough that she's stopped noticing. He looks out over the floor with her for a moment — the two of them just watching it hum.

“Emilia, plans are in place to convert the south lobby into an additional gaming room. I'd like your thoughts on which game to place in that room. Can you let me know within two days?”

“Yes, sir.”

He takes one more look across the floor, says “Very good” again to no one in particular, and heads for his next appointment. Emilia stands there a moment longer, running the two options through her head before she's even opened a spreadsheet — a room of card tables staffed by dealers, or a room of blinking machines that need no one at all.

Two days. She pulls out her phone and opens a blank note.

Background

Emilia Carlon has worked for Danny Paseronopolis, owner of The Landmark Hotel And Casino, in various roles for over ten years, including two years as the casino's manager — a position she took after a prior career as an actuarial consultant. The Landmark has operated for more than 20 years and was recently ranked among the top 3 hotels and casinos in the city. Mr. Paseronopolis has given Emilia two days to recommend which of two games to install in the newly available south lobby space.

Indigo

A dealer-managed card game, similar in structure to Poker or Blackjack. Players buy chips with their own money and bet on the strength of their hand; the casino takes a percentage of each pot — the pot rake — set by house policy (Appendix A). The south lobby can hold up to 4 tables, each seating up to 7 players and requiring one trained dealer.

Video Machines

Single-player, slot-style machines. A player bets a fixed amount and presses a button; the machine's built-in odds determine, at random, whether that turn wins. A losing turn splits the wagered amount between immediate casino revenue and a running jackpot pool (Appendix B). The south lobby can hold up to 25 machines, needing no dealer but requiring two security staff regardless of how many are in use.

The Landmark does not currently operate either game, so no historical revenue exists for this specific room. What Mr. Paseronopolis does have is two years of hour-by-hour player traffic from a comparable room at another property he owns, broken out by day of week and time of day (Appendix C), plus that same room's beverage-sales data by game type (Appendix D) — useful context for the pitch, though not required to answer the profit question Mr. Paseronopolis actually asked.

Neither game changes the cost of remodeling the south lobby itself — only the cost of the tables or machines and the staff who run them (Appendix A, Appendix B).

The vocabulary and "how to…" skills this case will ask you to research are listed in Appendix F — that is where Challenge 1 sends you.

Appendix E — Modeling Assumptions
These are stated scenario choices made explicit for this case, not figures found in Appendix A–D. You may challenge them in discussion — that is a legitimate move.
#AssumptionBasis
MA1The new room operates only during its peak traffic window, 16:00–04:00 daily (the two highest-traffic blocks in Appendix C), not around the clock.Appendix C's own numbers run meaningfully lower in every 04:00–16:00 block than in every 16:00–04:00 block; a brand-new room has no proven customer base to justify staffing it through its weakest hours from day one.
MA2The three jackpots in Appendix B are excluded from this case's weekly profit model.A single week is too short a window to responsibly estimate payout frequency at 125-to-1, 175-to-1, and 500-to-1 odds. Only the funding side (the 35% split) is modeled.
MA3Every table/machine installed is staffed and running throughout the hours the room is open (per MA1) — the room is modeled at its full stated maximum (4 tables; 25 machines).No other capacity figure is given in Appendix A or B.

    Working Notebook

    This is a calculator, not an answer key. Type your own numbers first — then hit Check to see if your arithmetic matches. It won't tell you which option to choose; that part is still yours.

    Step 1 — D1: Indigo weighted-average rake and game length (6–7 player tier)

    Weight each pot-size band's midpoint × rake % by that band's frequency and add them up; do the same with time per game. This is the tier that covers every 16:00–04:00 evening block in Appendix C.

    Step 2 — D3: Video machine blended profit per play

    Convert each "X to 1" odds figure to a win probability, find the casino's profit for a win and for a loss at each bet size, then weight by that bet size's frequency.

    Step 3 — D4: Peak-hours weekly profit, each option

    Under Appendix E (MA1–MA3): active tables/machines per block × your Step 1/2 rate × hours per week, minus staffing cost. Only the 16:00–04:00 blocks count.

    Twist

    Ask your facilitator before revealing this — in a live discussion, the Twist is dropped at a specific moment, not read in advance.

    Reveal the Twist

    Appendix A says plainly: "The casino does not have any Indigo tables currently," and "each Indigo table requires a trained dealer." Indigo is an entirely new game to The Landmark — it has never run a single table before.

    The facilitator reveals: The Landmark currently has exactly two dealers certified to run Indigo. The other two tables would sit empty, unstaffed, until new hires are trained and certified — which will not happen before opening night.
