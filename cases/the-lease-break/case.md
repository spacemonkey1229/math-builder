# Case Study: The Lease Break

MS | Math | Case Study | 2026-27 | Adventure

---

## Overview

Maya Ferris has two PDFs open on her laptop, side by side, and a cursor parked in neither. The coffee shop around her is emptying out; the barista has already flipped the chair onto the table two seats over. Her phone lights up again, screen-up this time on the table between the laptop and a half-eaten bagel: **can they push the contract a while longer? nothing confirmed yet — sorry.** It's the third version of that text in two weeks, and it still doesn't say yes.

She scrolls back up the first lease. Move-in cost due at signing sits in bold near the top, and just below it, in smaller print, the clause about what happens if she leaves before the term is up. She'd read that clause four times now. The second lease's number at the top is bigger. The clause below it is shorter.

Her leasing agent's email sits unanswered, subject line **Move forward today?** — the studio she's currently subletting turns over to its next tenant in days, and both landlords need a signed lease before end of business to hold the unit. Maya opens a blank spreadsheet in a third tab, types a column header, deletes it, and looks back at the two PDFs. She still hasn't decided which one to sign.

## Background

Maya Ferris is a contract quality-assurance tester on a fixed-term engagement with a software company two transit stops from downtown. Her contract is for a stated term with an option to extend that neither she nor her manager can confirm yet — extensions on her team have gone either way before (SEE APPENDIX C). She is responsible for her own housing budget and, because her current sublet is ending, for signing a new lease before her landlord's deadline (SEE APPENDIX C).

Two units are on the table, both move-in ready and both requiring a decision today. **The Cedarline** is a studio with the lower monthly rent of the two, but it carries a large non-refundable move-in fee due at signing and a steep early-termination penalty if Maya leaves before the lease's full term is up (SEE APPENDIX A). **The Aldergate** is a one-bedroom with a somewhat higher monthly rent, a smaller and more conventional security deposit, and a lease-break clause that lets her exit early for a stated buyout of one month's rent (SEE APPENDIX B).

Maya's contract situation is the reason this is a real decision and not an obvious one: if her contract runs its full stated term with no extension, she may need to move again the moment it ends; if it extends, she stays put for the long haul. Either lease is affordable against her stated monthly housing budget for a full year (SEE APPENDIX C), so the sticker-price comparison alone does not settle it — what settles it is what each lease actually costs *per month she actually lives there*, under both the scenario where she stays the full term and the scenario where she has to break the lease partway through.

Full source citations for every dollar figure in Appendices A and B are in Appendix D. The vocabulary and "how to…" skills this case will ask you to research are listed in Appendix E — that is where Challenge 1 sends you.

## Dilemma

You are Maya Ferris.

Do you:

**A.)** Sign **The Cedarline** — lower monthly rent, but a large non-refundable move-in fee and a steep early-termination penalty **OR**

**B.)** Sign **The Aldergate** — slightly higher monthly rent, a smaller security deposit, and a lease that can be broken early for one month's rent

## Modeling Assumptions

> invented for this scenario, so no real lease exists to quote directly. Each value below is a
> **stated scenario assumption**, chosen by the case author from within a real, cited market range
> or convention (see Appendix D for the underlying sources). A Hero may challenge any of these in
> discussion — that is a legitimate move.

| # | Assumption | Value used | Basis | Sensitivity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MA1 | The Cedarline's non-refundable move-in fee | $434 (≈30% of monthly rent) | Redfin reports typical non-refundable move-in fees at 20–50% of monthly rent [S3]. The case picks the upper-middle of that real range to embody the "large" fee the Background and Dilemma describe. | Amortized over 12 months (D2) this changes the effective monthly cost by only a few dollars either way — not dilemma-flipping on its own. |
| MA2 | The Cedarline's early-termination penalty | $5,065 (flat fee = 3.5 months' rent) | LeaseRunner's general lease-termination-fee explainer reports fees "typically 1-2 months' rent, and can reach 3-4 months in tight markets or when several months remain on the lease" [S8]; Landing's renter guide independently reports a 2-4 months' rent typical range [S7]. Maya's break-at-month-6 scenario is exactly the "several months remain on the lease" case both sources describe (6 of the Cedarline's 12-month term are still outstanding), so the case picks 3.5 months' rent — the middle of the higher, several-months-remaining sub-range both sources give — rather than the ordinary 1-2 month range that applies when a lease is nearly up anyway. | **This is the single most dilemma-relevant assumption in the case.** At month 6, this penalty is the largest single line item in The Cedarline's break-scenario cost (Appendix D3) — a Hero who argues for a milder penalty (closer to the ordinary 1-2 month range) is making a legitimate, discussable move, and should show how far the number would have to move before their recommendation changes. |
| MA3 | The Aldergate's refundable security deposit | $1,601 (= 1 month's rent) | A security deposit equal to one month's rent is the most common convention nationally; HUD's own Public Housing Occupancy Guidebook caps PHA deposits at one month's rent [S6], and most state landlord-tenant statutes use the same benchmark for private-market leases. The case states the Aldergate's deposit as this conventional figure, matching the Background's description of it as "smaller and more conventional." | Low — a refundable deposit is returned at move-out and does not compound with time the way a penalty does; it moves D2/D3's totals by a fixed, small amount in both scenarios. |
| MA4 | The Aldergate's early-termination buyout | $1,601 (= 1 month's rent) | LeaseRunner's cited real range for termination buyouts is 1–2 months' rent [S5]; the case states the Aldergate's clause at the bottom (most tenant-favorable) end of that same real range, matching the Background's description of a "stated buyout of one month's rent." | Moderate — this is the number that keeps The Aldergate's break-at-month-6 cost bounded and predictable; it is what makes Option B's early-exit risk capped rather than open-ended. |
| MA5 | Maya's contract's stated firm term | 6 months | Fixed-term QA/contract-testing engagements commonly run in 3, 6, or 12-month increments; the case fixes 6 months as a plausible mid-length engagement, short enough that an early lease break is a real possibility rather than a remote one. | Sets up MA6; if lengthened toward 12 months, the "break early" scenario becomes irrelevant and the case collapses to a single-scenario comparison. |
| MA6 | Month at which an early move would happen if the contract is not extended | Month 6 | Set equal to MA5's firm contract term — if the contract is not extended, Maya's engagement (and the reason she is renting nearby) ends exactly when the firm term ends. | Directly defines D3's amortization window; this is the number Challenge D3 names explicitly ("break at month 6"). |
| MA7 | Signing deadline | Today, end of business | Matches the Background's stated urgency ("both landlords need a signed lease before end of business to hold the unit"); a narrative constraint, not a figure that enters any calculation. | None — does not appear in D1–D5's arithmetic. |
| MA8 | Current sublet's move-out date | 3 days from today | Matches the Background's description ("the studio she's currently subletting turns over to its next tenant in days"); a narrative constraint, not a figure that enters any calculation. | None — does not appear in D1–D5's arithmetic. |
| MA9 | Maya's monthly housing budget ceiling | $2,450/month | Set comfortably above the most expensive computed scenario in this case (The Cedarline's break-at-month-6 effective monthly cost — see D3) so that, per the Background, "either lease is affordable against her stated monthly housing budget for a full year," making the effective-cost comparison (not affordability) the deciding factor. | None directly — this is a narrative device that rules out "just pick the cheaper sticker price" as a shortcut; it does not enter D2–D4's arithmetic. |
| MA10 | If Maya's contract extends, the length of that extension | Covers the remainder of the signed lease's full 12-month term | The case only defines two scenarios (D2: stay the full 12-month term; D3: break at month 6), matching the Background's binary framing ("if it extends, she stays put for the long haul"). This assumption states explicitly what that framing implies but never spelled out as a figure: an extension is long, not partial, so "extended" maps cleanly to D2 and "not extended" maps cleanly to D3 with no undefined middle case. | None directly — a narrative/scoping convention, not a number that enters D2–D4's arithmetic. It matters for discussion: without it stated, a student could ask "what if the extension is only a few months?", a scenario this case does not model. |

**Why the penalty uses S7/S8 instead of S5's narrower range:** S5 (LeaseRunner, a different article on
the same site) states a general 1-2 months' rent range for lease-termination fees and buyouts and
still grounds The Aldergate's buyout (MA4) and the *ordinary* case for The Cedarline's penalty. But
two independent sources — S7 and S8 — separately report that the real range widens to 2-4 months'
rent specifically when, as in Maya's break-at-month-6 scenario, several months still remain on the
lease at the time of the break. The Cedarline's penalty (MA2) uses that wider, scenario-matched range
rather than the general one.

**Note on S1/S2 being refreshed (2026-09-04):** RentCafe's page is a live-updating market-trends
dashboard, not a fixed historical snapshot, so the figures it shows change over time as new listing
data comes in. A routine data-decay check found the page had moved from $1,389/$1,536 (as of
2026-07-02) to $1,447/$1,601 (as of 2026-08-31) — this is normal drift for this kind of source, not
a sign the earlier figures were wrong. S1/S2 and every figure derived as a percent or multiple of
them (MA1, MA2, MA3, MA4, MA9) were refreshed to the current page values rather than left pointing
at a figure the live page no longer shows.

**Note on a figure deliberately not used:** several aggregator sites publish a national "average rent"
figure that blends studio, 1-bedroom, and larger units into one number; that blended figure is not the
bedroom-specific figure this case needs and was excluded rather than used as a proxy — see
`RESEARCH_LOG.md` for the two conflicting national figures found and why neither was used here.
