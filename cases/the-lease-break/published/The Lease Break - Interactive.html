Overview

Maya Ferris has two PDFs open on her laptop, side by side, and a cursor parked in neither. The coffee shop around her is emptying out; the barista has already flipped the chair onto the table two seats over. Her phone lights up again, screen-up this time on the table between the laptop and a half-eaten bagel: “can they push the contract a while longer? nothing confirmed yet — sorry.” It’s the third version of that text in two weeks, and it still doesn’t say yes.

She scrolls back up the first lease. Move-in cost due at signing sits in bold near the top, and just below it, in smaller print, the clause about what happens if she leaves before the term is up. She’d read that clause four times now. The second lease’s number at the top is bigger. The clause below it is shorter.

Her leasing agent’s email sits unanswered, subject line “Move forward today?” — the studio she’s currently subletting turns over to its next tenant in days, and both landlords need a signed lease before end of business to hold the unit. Maya opens a blank spreadsheet in a third tab, types a column header, deletes it, and looks back at the two PDFs. She still hasn’t decided which one to sign.

Background

Maya Ferris is a contract quality-assurance tester on a fixed-term engagement with a software company two transit stops from downtown. Her contract is for a stated term with an option to extend that neither she nor her manager can confirm yet — extensions on her team have gone either way before (see Appendix C). She is responsible for her own housing budget and, because her current sublet is ending, for signing a new lease before her landlord’s deadline (see Appendix C).

Two units are on the table, both move-in ready and both requiring a decision today. The Cedarline is a studio with the lower monthly rent of the two, but it carries a large non-refundable move-in fee due at signing and a steep early-termination penalty if Maya leaves before the lease’s full term is up (see Appendix A). The Aldergate is a one-bedroom with a somewhat higher monthly rent, a smaller and more conventional security deposit, and a lease-break clause that lets her exit early for a stated buyout of one month’s rent (see Appendix B).

Maya’s contract situation is the reason this is a real decision and not an obvious one: if her contract runs its full stated term with no extension, she may need to move again the moment it ends; if it extends, she stays put for the long haul. Either lease is affordable against her stated monthly housing budget for a full year (see Appendix C), so the sticker-price comparison alone does not settle it — what settles it is what each lease actually costs per month she actually lives there, under both the scenario where she stays the full term and the scenario where she has to break the lease partway through.

Full source citations for every dollar figure in Appendices A and B are in Appendix D. The vocabulary and “how to…” skills this case will ask you to research are listed in Appendix E — that is where Challenge 1 sends you.

    Working Notebook

    This is a calculator, not an answer key. Type your own numbers first — then hit Check to see if your arithmetic matches. It won't tell you which lease to sign; that part is still yours.

    Step 1 — D2: Stay full term (12 months)

    Amortize each lease's up-front move-in cost across its full 12-month term, then add it to monthly rent.

    Step 2 — D3: Break at month 6

    Amortize each lease's up-front cost and its early-termination penalty across only 6 months, then add to monthly rent.

    Step 3 — D4: Up-front cost as a percent of one month's rent

    Divide each lease's up-front move-in cost by its own monthly rent, then convert to a percent.

    Step 4 — Compare the two scenarios

    Use your Step 1 and Step 2 numbers: which lease is cheaper under each scenario, and by how much?

    Twist

    Ask your facilitator before revealing this — in a live discussion, the Twist is dropped at a specific moment, not read in advance.

    Reveal the Twist

    Maya's manager just called: her contract situation is now resolved, one way or the other — your facilitator will tell you which. If it's confirmed extended, it covers the rest of whichever lease's 12-month term Maya signs (Appendix C, MA10) — that maps to your Step 1 (stay-full-term) numbers. If it's confirmed not extended, Maya moves again at month 6 — that maps to your Step 2 (break-at-month-6) numbers.
