Appendix B — The Measured Current at Buoy 44090

NDBC reports ocean current direction as the direction the current is flowing toward (the

opposite convention from how NDBC reports wind, which is reported as the direction it blows

from) [S4]. Current speed is reported in centimeters per second.

Current speed, converted to knots27 cm/s ÷ 51.4444 cm/s per kt = 0.5248 kt (1 kt = 1 nm/hr = 1,852 m/hr = 51.4444 cm/s)

How to read this table for D1. A current "toward 340 at 0.5248 kt" is a vector of magnitude

0.5248 kt pointed on a bearing of 340° true. Resolved into east/north components (east =

magnitude × sin(bearing), north = magnitude × cos(bearing)): east ≈ −0.1795 kt, north ≈

+0.4932 kt — a westward-and-north-flowing set.

    Working Notebook

    This is a calculator, not an answer key. Type your own numbers first — then hit Check to see if your arithmetic matches. It won't tell you which option to choose; that part is still yours.

    Step 1 — Resolve the current vector into components (D1)

    Appendix B gives the real measured current's speed and direction it flows TOWARD. Using standard nautical-bearing components (x = speed × sin(bearing), y = speed × cos(bearing)), resolve the current into its x/y components, first converting its speed from cm/s to knots (Appendix B/S4, 1 kt = 51.4444 cm/s).

    Step 2 — Cost per nautical mile, both options (D2–D5)

    Option A: subtract the current vector from the required over-ground velocity vector to find the boat's own required heading vector, then its speed — look up the fuel rate at that speed (Appendix C) and compute cost per nm. Option B: hold Appendix C's economical speed and heading, use the dot product to split the resultant velocity into along-track and cross-track components, find the extra distance/time the cross-track drift costs, and compute cost per nm the same way.

    Does Option B's schedule still make the weather window, or does the cross-track drift push it past 3 hours?

    Twist

    Ask your facilitator before revealing this — in a live discussion, the Twist is dropped at a specific moment, not read in advance.

    Reveal the Twist

    New forecast update: the closing weather window isn't 3.0 hours anymore — conditions are worsening faster than expected, and the window is now 2.0 hours.

    Recompute Step 2 for both options under the tighter window. Does the $/nm winner change? What happens to Option B's chance of actually making it in time?
