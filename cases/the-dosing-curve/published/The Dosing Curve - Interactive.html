Overview

Dr. Priya Chandrasekaran holds two dosing charts under the barn's single working light, and neither one is going away.

The stocker-cattle operation's foreman waits by the chute, breath fogging, boots already caked from morning treatment rounds up and down pen alleys where a fresh batch of calves came in coughing off the truck. Bacterial pneumonia is moving through the pen faster than paperwork can keep up, and whatever protocol Priya signs tonight becomes the herd's official treatment record — the one a USDA auditor, an antimicrobial-stewardship reviewer, or the operation's own insurer could pull a year from now and ask her to defend.

Her phone buzzes on the tailgate: a text from the owner, one line, no punctuation, asking what this is going to cost. She doesn't answer yet. On her laptop, two drug labels sit open side by side — the same active ingredient, two different regimens. One calls for a single injection, done and forgotten by morning. The other asks for repeated shots spread across the week, cheaper per dose but requiring the crew back in that chute again and again, calves handled and stressed each time.

Neither dosing chart, by itself, tells her whether the herd's actual cumulative drug exposure clears the published efficacy line before the drug's effect fades between doses — that only shows up in the shape of the curve, not in the number of shots. The foreman raps twice on the rail. Priya closes one laptop tab, and hovers the cursor over the other.

Background

Dr. Priya Chandrasekaran is a food-animal veterinary pharmacology consultant retained by the operation to design and sign the herd's antimicrobial treatment protocol for an active bacterial-respiratory-disease outbreak. Her signature makes the protocol the herd's official, auditable treatment record; whichever regimen she approves must simultaneously clear a real, published pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic efficacy threshold and remain defensible to the operation's owner on cost and animal-handling grounds. A chart that clears the efficacy line but blows past the handling budget fails her review, and so does a chart that is cheap but cannot be shown to hold the drug's exposure above the line for the full treatment window.

The Single Shot is one long-acting intramuscular injection, given once, at a labeled dose of 9 mg of oxytetracycline per lb body weight (see Appendix D).

The Four-Day Course is repeated conventional-formulation intramuscular injections, given once daily at a labeled dose of 3–5 mg of oxytetracycline per lb body weight per day for up to 4 consecutive days (see Appendix D).

Published single-dose pharmacokinetic data (see Appendix A) show the two formulations reach total single-dose exposure areas within a small margin of each other despite the long-acting formulation's much longer half-life and lower peak concentration — a difference in curve shape, not in total dose, that a single number cannot resolve. Sampled concentration-time data for both formulations (see Appendix B) supply the raw points a Riemann sum needs to estimate that curve's area directly; a fitted continuous model of the same data supplies the function a definite integral needs to check it.

Whichever option Priya signs must clear a real, published minimum ratio of free-drug cumulative exposure to the outbreak pathogen's minimum inhibitory concentration (fAUC/MIC), checked against a named veterinary susceptibility breakpoint for that pathogen (see Appendix C), across the full treatment window — not just at one moment. Cost and handling are not decorative: the operation is working against a fixed per-head treatment budget, and every extra handling event carries a real, industry-documented injection-site-lesion cost the owner has asked to see priced out, not waved away (see Appendix E). The pre-slaughter withdrawal period is identical for both formulations and does not distinguish them (see Appendix D). The vocabulary and "how to…" skills this case will ask of you are listed in Appendix H — that is where Challenge 1 sends you — and every source is cited in full in Appendix F.

    Appendix G — Convention-Sensitivity Inputs (for D5)

    Both formulations' modeled curves in Appendix B were reconstructed from Aktaş & Yarsan 2017's real study dose — 20 mg/kg IM, single dose [S1], which converts to ≈9.07 mg/lb — not from the Four-Day Course's actual labeled dose (3–5 mg/lb/day [S5]). Appendix B's curve therefore has to be rescaled by dose for the Four-Day Course before D3's fAUC/MIC check means anything for the label's real dosing rate (linear pharmacokinetics is already assumed throughout this case, per MA2, so AUC scales linearly with dose). The Single Shot's labeled dose (9 mg/lb [S4]) is already almost identical to the study dose, so no rescaling is needed there.

    Two real, genuinely competing MIC benchmarks (the fAUC/MIC denominator):

    BenchmarkSourceValue usedResulting 24-h target
    Winter et al. 2024 population PK/PD cutoff, calves[S2]1 mg/L24 × 1 = 24 µg·h/mL
    CLSI VET01S clinical breakpoint, susceptible[S3]≤2 mg/L24 × 2 = 48 µg·h/mL

    Winter et al.'s adult-cattle cutoff is also 2 mg/L — numerically identical to the CLSI breakpoint here — so these two sources converge on the same 48 µg·h/mL target whenever the non-calves value is used. The calves value, 1 mg/L → 24 µg·h/mL, is the only benchmark that differs numerically from the other two.

    Two window conventions, applied to the Four-Day Course only (the Single Shot is one injection — a single continuous definite integral, so there is no "gap between doses" for a window convention to distinguish):

    • Day-1-only: the first day's AUC0–24h alone, with no benefit yet from later doses stacking up — the strictest, worst-case reading of "does every 24-h window clear the target."
    • Course-cumulative (steady state): once MA2's multi-dose superposition is applied, each day's daily AUC rises toward a steady-state value scaled by the standard linear-PK accumulation factor R = 1 / (1 − e^(−ke·τ)), using ke = ln(2)/half-life and τ = the 24-h dosing interval. With the conventional formulation's real half-life (10.47 h [S1]), R ≈ 1.257 for a 24-h interval — a real, standard pharmacokinetic formula, not a fitted or fabricated number.

    Working Notebook

    This is a calculator, not an answer key. Type your own numbers first — then hit Check to see if your arithmetic matches. It won't tell you which option to sign; that part is still yours.

    Step 1 — Riemann sum for AUC0–24h (D1)

    Using Appendix B's own real sampling timepoints from 0 through 24 h (treat C(0)=0, the moment of injection), build a trapezoidal Riemann sum: for each subinterval, average the two endpoint concentrations and multiply by the subinterval's width (Δt), then add every subinterval's contribution. Pick an option, compute your sum, and enter it below.

    This is exactly the computation behind case-readiness's own primary-gate resolution — the model doesn't hand you the pass/fail, only the AUC number, and only once you've entered your own.

    Step 2 — Antiderivative and FTC for a fitted C(t) (D2)

    This notebook fits one reasonable least-squares quadratic C(t) = at² + bt + c through the chosen option's Appendix B points over 0–24 h. Write the general antiderivative F(t) using the power rule (don't forget the constant of integration is irrelevant once you subtract), then evaluate the definite integral over [0, 24] by the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.

    Compare this FTC value to your Step 1 sum for the same option. They will not match exactly — a smooth least-squares curve cannot pass through every real sampled point, including the sharp early rise near t=0.25–0.5 h. That gap is real information about the model, not an arithmetic mistake in either calculation.

    Step 3 — fAUC/MIC comparison (D3)

    Appendix C's target is fAUC24h/MIC = 24 (target hours × 1). Using your own AUC0–24h from Step 1 or Step 2, and the MIC cutoff for the population you believe applies (Appendix C), compute the ratio and enter it below. The notebook will tell you whether your own entered ratio clears the target — it does not tell you which MIC cutoff is right, or announce a verdict for the case as a whole.

    Step 4 — Shaded AUC graph and per-animal cost comparison (D4)

    Using Appendix D/E and Appendix H's MA3/MA4, compute each option's total per-animal cost: drug cost (mL required × price per mL) plus expected injection-site-lesion cost (handling events × per-lesion cost, scaled from the 1995 incidence rate to the current 2022 incidence rate). Try the slider to see how MA4's dosing-rate choice, within the label's real 3–5 mg/lb/day range, moves the picture — this is exactly what Tier 2 probe 5 checks mechanically against sensitivity_model.py.
