Overview

"Moving on to our next order of business," states the Chairman, presiding over the weekly commission hearing. "We have before us two bids for the off-site, long-term storage of high-level radioactive waste from Region Three."

Two CEOs sit at the witness table, each representing a company that wants the contract. For the next two and a half hours, the commissioners work through the bids line by line — storage method, safety record, cost — while the room listens.

As the questioning wraps up, the Chairman looks down the row of commissioners. "Thank you both for your time today. We'll review these bids and announce our decision at the next session."

The room empties. The commissioners are left with two folders, two very different storage plans, and a decision that has to hold up for decades. Whatever they sign today, someone will still be living with it long after every person in this room has retired.

Background

The commission is an independent federal agency tasked with protecting public health and safety related to nuclear energy. It is awarding a contract to store spent Uranium-235 reactor rods from 15 different sites across Region Three, covering 250,000 spent fuel rods, each a cylindrical container.

Isolation International, headquartered in Decatur, Illinois, stores waste above ground in cylindrical barrels, laid out in rows and never stacked. The company has operated its facility since 1984 and holds a 4.8 out of 5 safety rating. Every barrel is fully sealed and lined on the inside — top, bottom, and the curved side wall — with a radiation-shielding coating, at a real, disclosed cost per square foot of surface area.

NuStorage, LLC, headquartered in Grand Rapids, Michigan, stores waste below ground in cube-shaped crates (a rectangular prism with all sides equal), stacked up to three high, at a facility just off the shore of Lake Huron built in 2011. NuStorage holds a 4.5 out of 5 safety rating.

Both companies are experienced, licensed, and highly regarded. The commission's job is to weigh what each one actually offers.

    Pick a company. Compare rod volume to container volume (cylinder for the barrel, cube for the crate).

    Step 2 — Total first-year cost for 250,000 rods (D2)

    Step 3 — Maximum facility capacity (D3)

    Find the same company's maximum number of containers the facility can hold.

    Step 4 — Safety rating gap (D4)

    Step 5 — Barrel surface area and lining cost (D6)

    One Isolation barrel's total interior surface area (both circular ends plus the curved side wall — it's fully sealed), then the lining cost for one barrel and for all the barrels this contract needs.

    Twist

    Ask your facilitator before revealing this — in a live discussion, the Twist is dropped at a specific moment, not read in advance.

    Reveal the Twist

    Transportation from the reactor sites to whichever facility you chose is done by rail. That railroad passes through two major urban centers along the way.

    This twist doesn't change any of your numbers — it asks whether population density along the transport route belongs in your safety assessment at all, even though neither company's safety rating accounts for it directly.
