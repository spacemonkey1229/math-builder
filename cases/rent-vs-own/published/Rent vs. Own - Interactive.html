Overview

"Taylor, I just got a call from our landlords. They're going to list the house for sale at the end of our lease," Megan tells her husband. Shocked, he responds, "That gives us only five weeks to find a new place!"

"They actually said they'd give us priority if we want to buy it," Megan replies.

"I don't know if it's worth it — it's a small house, and we've been talking about needing more room for the kids," Taylor says.

"Plus the market is nuts right now. I don't know if we could even find another rental at the price we're paying, let alone what buying would cost," Megan worries. "But if we don't move, and the market keeps climbing the way it has been..."

She pulls out her laptop and starts a new spreadsheet. "They said they'd send the purchase numbers over today. Let's actually run this before we decide anything."

Background

Megan and Taylor Newman have three children and have rented the same single-family home in Boise, Idaho for three years. They've been building savings toward eventually owning a home but have never bought one before.

Their current rental is 1,376 square feet with three bedrooms and two bathrooms. If they rent again, they'd want a comparably sized home in the same area. Earlier this year they completed mortgage pre-qualification through their bank: a minimum down payment of 2.5% and closing costs of about 3.5% of the purchase price. Conventional guidance recommends spending no more than 28% of gross monthly income on housing and no more than 36% on total debt combined; their bank told them they could qualify to borrow up to 37% of gross income on the mortgage itself.

Because the Newmans have been reliable tenants, their landlords are offering to sell them the current rental for $360,000 — $15,000 below the price they'd list it for otherwise. Boise home values have been rising sharply, and market forecasts point to continued double-digit growth this year.
