# Guide Notes: Rent vs. Own

**Case:** Rent vs. Own · **Tier:** T3 · **Band:** MS-stretch/HS-entry · **Studio:** Heroes Academy
**Standards:** see `case.json.standards[]` for the exact ids (9 skills — exponents, order of

## Intro

Frame the format: this is a real family finance decision under a real deadline, not a worksheet.
Name the opener — "Five weeks to decide, three kids, and a mortgage formula that doesn't care how
you feel about either option."

## Launch

Read the rent total ($1,512.21/month) and the mortgage total ($1,789.83/month) aloud, with no
percentages computed yet. Ask: "Just from the two dollar totals, does this look like an easy call
or a close one?" Do not confirm or correct answers here.

## Anchor 1 — Take a stand and vote

**TAKE A STAND AND VOTE. No reasoning yet, just commit.**

"Who rents? Who buys?" Show of hands, no discussion. Record the count — Close will ask them to vote
again.

## Anchor 2 — What is the problem we're solving?

**"Is this the right question?" · "Is it just as simple as which costs less, or is there something
else to consider?"** (the original Guide Notes' own language, preserved)

Let confusion grow here, deliberately. Push on the guideline tension: "The bank says the Newmans
qualify. Conventional wisdom says they'd be stretched. Which one is actually the constraint that
matters?"

## Anchor 3 — What is the process?

**"What's the process we're going to go through? If we end up with ___ of this and ___ of that,
how will we make our final decision?"** (also the original's own language)

Once the room has named its real deciding factor(s), ask them to name the actual steps: total the
rent cost, compute the down payment and closing costs, run the mortgage formula, add the bundled
extras, and check both totals against the 28%/36%/37% guidelines. Re-ask: "Do we agree that's the
right process?"

## Anchor 4 — What don't we know?

**"Is there anything we don't know that would be critical? Do we need to make assumptions?"**
(also the original's own language)

Surface Appendix B's two real conventions: the stated 3.0% mortgage rate and the bundled
PMI/tax/insurance estimate are both real, disclosed approximations, not the Newmans' exact
paperwork. Ask which one actually moves the final numbers more if it's off.

## Data Deep Dive

Now, and only now, the numbers. Walk D3's mortgage formula step by step as a room, cold-calling for
the exponent step specifically — this is where a Hero who evaluated (1+r)^n incorrectly (dividing
instead of raising to a power, or using the annual rate instead of the monthly rate) will get a
visibly wrong payment.

## Twist — drop this at ~15 minutes into the discussion

Deliver slowly, with pauses:

"The bank calls. Rates moved before you could lock one in — 3.5% now, not 3.0%. Does your
recommendation still hold?"

**Facilitator-only: what the numbers actually do (`case.json.derived`).** The monthly mortgage
payment rises from $1,479.83 to $1,576.15; total monthly owning cost rises from $1,789.83 to
$1,886.15 — now **36.06%** of gross income, brushing right up against the 36% total-debt guideline
on housing costs alone. **The twist does not flip the verdict — renting was already cheaper, and
this widens the gap** — but it pushes buying from "above the 28% guideline" to "nearly at the 36%
guideline by itself," a real, meaningfully different risk picture. Ask: "Does a rate that moves
before closing change how confident you'd be recommending 'buy' at all?"

## Immediate recommit

Re-vote immediately. Ask by name: "Did the rate change your recommendation, or just how strongly
you'd make it?"

## Deep conflict — three threads (20-30 min)

1. **The bank's limit vs. the conventional guideline.** US Bank would approve up to 37% on the
   mortgage alone; conventional guidance caps housing at 28%. Whose number should the Newmans
   actually trust more?
2. **Cash today vs. equity tomorrow.** Renting keeps $21,600 in the bank; buying forecasts over 10%
   appreciation this year on a home priced below market. How do you weigh a guaranteed cash
   cushion against a real but uncertain market forecast?
3. **What "affordable" means with three kids.** The DTI guidelines are generic; the Newmans have
   specific, real obligations (childcare, growth room) the ratios don't capture. Should a family
   with kids budget more conservatively than the guideline suggests, or is the guideline already
   built for exactly this?

## The trap

A Hero computes one monthly total, compares it to the other, and stops — never checking either
option against the 28%/36%/37% guidelines at all. The case's real tension is that "cheaper" and
"within guideline" are two different questions, and a family can pass one test and fail the other.
Watch for a pitch that reports a dollar comparison and calls the case closed.

## Socratic questions

1. Renting costs 18.36% less than buying per month — how would you explain to Megan and Taylor why that gap is or isn't decisive on its own?
2. Buying would put the Newmans at 34.22% of income on housing — above the 28% conventional guideline, but under the bank's own 37% mortgage limit. Whose number should carry more weight, and why?
3. The Newmans have $33,259.75 in checking and savings against the $21,600 needed at closing -- an $11,659.75 cushion. How should that cushion factor into how risky Option B feels, compared to a family buying at the exact edge of their cash?
4. The mortgage formula requires evaluating (1.0025)^360 — why does a 30-year loan term matter as an exponent, not just a number you multiply by?
5. After the twist, owning costs 36.06% of income — one hundredth of a percentage point from the 36% conventional debt guideline. Does landing that close to a guideline number feel meaningfully different from landing comfortably under or over it?

## Close

**VOTE AGAIN.** "Did we ask the right question at the start?" "What math was new to you tonight, and
how did you actually attack it?" Compare the closing vote to Anchor 1's — a room that didn't move at
all after the twist is worth asking about directly: did the rate change not feel real, or did the
room already expect buying to get more expensive, not less?
