Overview

The generator dies at 4:40 in the morning, and in the sudden quiet Adaeze Okonkwo can hear the rain starting on the storeroom's tin roof.

She does not move for a moment. Behind her, in the dark, the walk-in refrigerator holds every dose of measles vaccine the district will get this quarter, already three weeks late. The backup generator will hold. The backup generator has always held.

Her phone lights the room green as she checks it. Twenty-two minutes until the drivers arrive. Somewhere out past the district line, in six villages strung along a week of bad road that turns to soup when it rains like this, mothers have already started walking toward clinics that have had nothing to offer them since March.

On the desk in front of her are two purchase orders, both approved, both stamped, and she is allowed to submit exactly one. One buys four small cold boxes, light enough that a single health worker can strap one to the back of a motorcycle and take it up the footpaths to Bulera and Nyamirama, where no vehicle has ever gone. The other buys two large ones — far cheaper for every litre they carry, and far too heavy for anything but the truck.

The truck cannot reach Bulera. The truck has never reached Nyamirama.

Adaeze picks up both forms, one in each hand, and outside she hears the first engine turning over in the yard.

Background

Adaeze Okonkwo is the District Vaccine Logistics Officer for Gomba District. She is responsible for getting vaccine from the district cold store to six rural health facilities — and for every dose that spoils on the way. She reports quarterly on doses administered and doses wasted, and both numbers follow her.

Quick facts
This quarter's allocation9,000 doses
Container budget$420
Facilities on the circuit6
Safe vaccine range+2°C to +8°C
Cold life measured at+43°C ambient, lid closed, to +10°C

Once the vaccine leaves the district cold store, it is travelling in passive containers — insulated boxes chilled with frozen water-packs. There is no refrigeration on the vehicles. The cold is packed in at the start and it runs out.

The World Health Organization certifies these containers by a measurement called cold life: the number of hours from the moment the lid closes until the warmest point inside first reaches +10°C, tested at a constant outside temperature of +43°C (see Appendix A). Measles vaccine must be held between +2°C and +8°C; the target is the midpoint of that range. Any reading outside the range is a temperature excursion, and excursed doses cannot be administered (see Appendix B).

Order A — The Light Fleet

Four AOV ACB-264SL cold boxes, $102.21 each — $408.84 total. 6 litres of vaccine capacity per box, 25.4 kg fully loaded. Adaeze's team plans to send three boxes on the truck route and one box on the motorcycle route (Bulera and Nyamirama). At 25.4 kg, one worker can strap a box to a motorcycle — compare that against the WHO lifting guidance in Appendix A before you decide how comfortable you are with it.

Order B — The Heavy Pair

Two Blowkings CB-20-CF cold boxes, $128.00 each — $256.00 total. 20 litres of vaccine capacity per box, 49.5 kg fully loaded — far cheaper per litre than Order A, but each box needs two people and a vehicle. Both boxes go on the truck route. Bulera and Nyamirama receive nothing.

The circuit and the six facilities' demand are in Appendix C. Container specs are in Appendix A. Vaccine packing volumes and prices are in Appendix D. Temperature excursion: "Any temperature reading outside ranges recommended in the vaccine manufacturers' package inserts is considered a temperature excursion and requires immediate action."

The 8-hour figure limits a single clinic working session, not transport time. A multi-day outreach circuit is governed by the container's cold life, not by this number. If you call the target temperature T, an excursion is any moment when the reading differs from T by more than 3°C — there's a compact way to write that with a single expression (Appendix G).

Heat enters a cold box through its walls. The bigger the wall area, the more heat gets in. The bigger the inside, the more cold is stored to absorb it.

The water content of the 0.4 L pack used in the AOV and Blowkings boxes is not published. The 0.3 L and 0.6 L packs both fill below their nominal capacity, so don't assume 0.4 L exactly — if your calculation needs it, state what you assumed and why.

Appendix F — Modeling Assumptions

Assumption 0 — Box assignment. Order A's four boxes are assigned by vehicle, not by facility: three boxes travel the truck route and serve all four road-accessible facilities between them; one box travels the motorcycle route and serves both Bulera and Nyamirama. Order B's two boxes both travel the truck route. Use this for every calculation.

How the three truck boxes split the four truck stops among themselves does not matter — even the worst case (one box absorbing all four stops) still clears the last stop with 20.3 hours to spare.

Assumption 1 — Opening cost. Assume each lid opening costs 4 hours of cold life, deducted up front for every stop a box is planned to make before it ever leaves the depot. This case's headline numbers are stable for any opening cost from 2 to 6 hours — try it yourself in the Working Notebook below.

Assumption 2 — Holding, then failing. Treat cold life as the in-spec time: vaccine arriving at or before the box's remaining cold life is in spec; vaccine arriving after it is an excursion.

This is a simplification, and it is the optimistic direction — a real box drifts upward through the +8°C line somewhat before it reaches +10°C. If you think that matters, model it and show what changes. A student who argues that both orders are worse than they look, and shows the arithmetic, is making a stronger case than one who doesn't notice.
