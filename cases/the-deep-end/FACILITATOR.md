# Guide Notes: The Deep End

*Facilitator-only. Do not paste any of this into case.md's student-facing appendices — the
balance argument, margin, and sensitivity notes live only here and in `case.json`'s `balance`
object.*

**Case:** The Deep End · **Tier:** T1 · **Band:** MS-entry · **Studio:** MS Adventure
**Standards:** Number Sense — `number-sense-depth` (n=0, new coverage), `number-sense-digit`
(n=0, new coverage), `number-sense-how-to-compare-two-or-more-values` (already well covered, reinforcement).

**A note on scope, read this before running the room:** this is deliberately a lighter, early-track
case. Both new-coverage skills are basic elementary vocabulary *terms* — "Depth" and "Digit" — not
multi-step procedures, so the numeric load here is genuinely simple: two short reading-and-labeling
tasks (D1, D2) plus one division-and-comparison task (D3). Do not pad the discussion out to feel
like a T3/T4 case. The Socratic plan below is scaled down accordingly — the "deep conflict" section
is a single thread, not two or three, because there is only one real thread to pull on at this
level.

**Status:** Researched 2026-09-05. Numbers below are the real, sourced/stated figures — never state
them, or the margin's size/direction, inside `case.md`.

## The real numbers (facilitator reference — never appears in case.md)

- Rate_A = 45 hrs/week ÷ 2,700 sq ft = 0.016667 hours per square foot (Deep-End Design).
- Rate_B = 63 hrs/week ÷ 3,500 sq ft = 0.018 hours per square foot (Zero-Entry Ramp Design).
- **Margin: 8.0% in the Zero-Entry Ramp Design's favor** (`case.json.margin_pct` = 8.0, well
  inside the SPEC §2 20% cap).
- **Why this stays a genuine two-sided case, not a landslide:** the Deep-End Design's *absolute*
  footprint (2,700 sq ft) and construction cost are meaningfully smaller (23% less basin area), and
  it is the only design that gives the swim team a real, exclusive practice window. The ramp's
  advantage is real and computed, but it is a rate advantage bought with meaningfully more basin
  area to build and maintain (compare the two footprints in Appendix A/B) — both arguments are
  quantitatively live.
- **This margin is genuinely fragile — say so, don't defend 8.0% as a stable fact.** Tier 2 Probe 5
  swept all four stated assumptions (`total_weekly_hours`, `reserved_hours_a`, `footprint_a`,
  `footprint_b`) at ±50% and found the winner flips, or the margin blows past the SPEC §2 20% cap,
  under every single one of them — e.g. a 50% *smaller* Deep-End footprint (1,350 sq ft) flips the
  winner back to the Deep-End Design at an 85% margin; a 50% *larger* reserved-practice block (27
  hrs/week) pushes the Ramp's margin to 35%. This is disclosed, not eliminated, in
  `case.json.sensitivity_disclosure[]` (all four entries are Category X: no engineering or staffing
  plan exists yet for either design, since the rebuild has not broken ground — every one of these
  four numbers is a documented good-faith scenario estimate, not a published fact about this
  specific facility). Do not let a Hero (or yourself) treat 8.0% as solid ground; treat it as "the
  best estimate given numbers nobody has actually finalized yet," which is the honest position.

## Intro

Frame the format before anything else: this is a real council vote in two weeks, not a worksheet —
whichever design the room recommends is the one that gets built, with no do-over. Name the opener:
"You're Carla Whitfield. The council votes on your recommendation. There's no third design and no
'let's revisit it next year.'" Let the two stakeholders in the Overview — the swim-team parent and
the wheelchair-using resident — both land before moving to the Launch.

## Launch

Read the two design nicknames out loud with no numbers attached yet — the Deep-End Design and the
Zero-Entry Ramp Design. Ask: "If all you knew was those two names and the two people who spoke at
the meeting, which side would you be on right now, and why?" Take answers without correcting
anyone. Most rooms will split along a "the team earned that pool" vs. "everyone should be able to
get in" line — record that split, because the case's job is to replace both gut reactions with an
actual number.

## Anchor 1 — Take a stand and vote

**TAKE A STAND AND VOTE. No reasoning yet, just commit.**

"Is anyone courageous enough to stand with the Zero-Entry Ramp Design — the one with no diving
well?" Show of hands, both options, no discussion. Record the count — Close will ask again.

## Anchor 2 — What is the problem we're solving?

**"Is 'which design has more square feet' the right question?" · "What do you mean by
'accessible' — accessible to whoever's already there, or accessible at every scheduled hour?"**

Push the room to state the actual metric before touching an appendix: not "which design is bigger"
and not "which design costs less," but supervised swim-hours delivered to the pool's actual user
base **per square foot of footprint it uses**. If the room lands on "we can't compare these by
total hours or total footprint alone — we need a rate," the case is working as designed.

## Anchor 3 — What is the process?

**"If we go through this process and find the data, how will we decide?"**

Make the room name the steps before opening an appendix: (1) read each design's posted depth
figures correctly — whole feet for the gauge, tenths of a foot for the ramp markers, (2) find each
design's weekly hours actually open to the general supervised user base, (3) find each design's
footprint, (4) divide hours by footprint for each design, (5) compare the two rates and recommend.
Re-ask: "Now that we can see the steps, is our question still the right one?"

## Anchor 4 — What don't we know?

**"Do we need to make assumptions? Which ones are critical?"**

Surface the honest gap: Appendix C's weekly-hours figures and both footprints are stated modeling
assumptions, not sourced numbers — no engineering drawing or staffing plan exists yet for either
design. Ask: "If the ramp's footprint were smaller than assumed, or the reserved swim-team block
were shorter, would that change which design wins on this rate — or just how close the two designs
are?" A sharp Hero should be able to say it could genuinely go either way; this case's own two
Modeling Assumptions are the most defensible numbers in it for a Hero to push back on.

## Data Deep Dive

Now, and only now, open Appendices A–C and work D1–D3. Cold-call for D1 and D2 first — a Hero who
reports a ramp marker as "2.6" but cannot say what the 6 means (six-tenths of a foot beyond the
2-foot mark) has not actually done D2's second half, only copied a number.

**Facilitator-only arithmetic note.** Deep-End accessible hours: 63 − 18 = 45 hrs/week. Rate_A =
45 / 2,700 = 0.016667 hrs/sq ft. Rate_B = 63 / 3,500 = 0.018 hrs/sq ft. Full derivation in
`case.json.balance.margin_basis`.

## Twist

**Twist: N/A.** This case's tension is a genuine, already-fully-disclosed two-variable trade-off
(a smaller footprint with a reserved-use block vs. a larger footprint with no reserved-use block)
that resolves entirely through the division-and-comparison the Challenges already require — there
is no additional real-world fact withheld from Appendices A–C whose reveal at the 15-minute mark
would invalidate a popular position without either (a) leaking the margin's size/direction into
case.md, which the case-write skill's balance rule forbids, or (b) inventing a number the research
pass didn't source, which this case does not do. The Data Deep Dive's own D3 rate reveal (many
rooms expect the smaller-footprint design to automatically be "more efficient"; the ramp design is
actually the higher rate once the reserved hours are subtracted) already produces the
surprise-and-recompute moment a Twist is meant to create, without adding new information — see the
Immediate Recommit note below, which the facilitator should treat as carrying that weight instead.
Given this is deliberately a lighter, early-track case, adding a second withheld fact on top of the
rate reveal would over-complicate a case that is supposed to stay simple.

## Immediate recommit

Right after D3's two rates land on the board, ask by name: "The Zero-Entry Ramp actually comes out
ahead on hours per square foot, even though it costs more to build — did you expect that before you
did the division? Does it change your vote?" Many Heroes who voted Deep-End at Anchor 1 on a
"smaller footprint must be more efficient" instinct will not have accounted for the reserved
practice block; conversely, a Hero who voted Ramp expecting it to "obviously" win by a landslide
should recompute and notice the margin is a real but modest 8%, not a rout.

## Deep conflict — one thread

**What the rate does and doesn't decide.** Push past "the ramp wins the math, so that's the
recommendation" to the harder, honest point: the swim team is a real, existing group of students
who will lose their only dedicated practice window under Option B, and the council has to weigh
that loss against the ramp's real efficiency and accessibility advantage. Ask: "The numbers say the
ramp delivers more supervised swim-hours per square foot to the general user base. Does that settle
the question by itself, or does Carla still have a judgment call to make?" There is no single
correct answer; the point is that the rate makes the trade-off precise, it does not remove the
trade-off.

| 15:00–20:00 | Immediate recommit — does the exact rate change anyone's vote |
| 20:00–35:00 | Deep conflict, one thread — does the rate settle the question, or is there still a judgment call |

## Close

**VOTE AGAIN.** "Same show of hands — Deep-End or Zero-Entry Ramp. Who moved, and why?" Then ask:
"Did we ask the right question at the start, or did it take Anchor 2 to find it?" And: "What math
was new to you today, and how did you attack it?" Listen specifically for whether anyone can state,
unprompted, that reading a decimal depth marker correctly (naming the tenths digit) was a genuinely
different skill from reading a whole-foot gauge, not just "the same thing with more decimals."

## Lessons Learned

- A smaller footprint is not automatically a more efficient use of space — you have to know what
  fraction of the time that footprint is actually usable by the people it is meant to serve.
- Reading a measurement correctly (a whole number vs. a decimal to a stated place) is not just a
  formatting detail — a gauge that can't show tenths of a foot is making a real design trade-off,
  not just a display choice.
- A rate (hours per square foot) can make two very different-looking designs directly comparable in
  a way that neither raw total (hours alone, or footprint alone) can.

## Standards Touched On

- **Depth** (`number-sense-depth`) — reading and labeling a whole-number depth value; identifying a
  maximum depth from a set of readings.
- **Digit** (`number-sense-digit`) — naming the digit in a specific place (the tenths place) of a
  decimal measurement and stating what real-world quantity it represents.
- **How to compare two or more values** (`number-sense-how-to-compare-two-or-more-values`) —
  computing and comparing a derived rate (hours per square foot) between two options to support a
  recommendation.

## The trap

The plausible wrong path: a Hero sees that the Deep-End Design has a *smaller* footprint (2,700 vs.
3,500 sq ft) and concludes it must be the more space-efficient choice without ever computing a rate
— or compares the two designs' *raw* weekly hours (45 vs. 63) and picks the ramp for "obviously more
hours" without ever dividing by footprint at all. Either shortcut skips the actual metric the case
asks for (hours **per square foot**) and can land on either answer for the wrong reason. Watch for a
pitch that never actually shows a division step — that is the tell that a Hero eyeballed the two
appendices instead of computing the rate.

## Five Socratic questions

1. Your Appendix A gauge only reports whole feet — never a fraction. What real trade-off does that design choice reflect, and would you want a diving well's depth reported any other way?
2. In your Appendix B table, what does the 6 in a 2.6-foot reading actually mean, in inches or in words — and would a swimmer read that marker differently if it were reported as "2 feet" instead?
3. The Deep-End Design has a smaller footprint. Why doesn't that alone make it the more space-efficient design once you compute the rate in D3?
4. Your D3 rate favors the ramp by a real but modest margin. What would have to change in Appendix C — the reserved hours, or either footprint — for that to flip, and how confident are you in those two numbers?
5. If the council could only afford to build the smaller footprint, is the swim team's exclusive practice window worth more to this city than the ramp's extra accessible hours? Is that a math question or a values question — and how do you tell the difference?
