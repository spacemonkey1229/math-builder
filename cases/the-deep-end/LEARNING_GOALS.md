**Forces test, applied to each mapping:**

- **D1 → Depth.** A student cannot produce a correct depth-reading table without reading and
  reporting a *depth* — the deliverable's whole point is transcribing the gauge's whole-foot depth
  values and correctly labeling which one is the pool's *maximum depth*. A student who does not
  understand "depth" as a specific, labeled quantity (as opposed to, say, length or width) cannot
  tell which of the three readings answers "what is the maximum depth."
- **D2 → Depth, Digit.** The deliverable is explicitly two things: recording each *depth* (to the
  tenth of a foot) and then naming the *digit* in the tenths place of that depth and what it means.
  A student could theoretically copy the five numbers without understanding "depth" as a concept —
  but cannot complete the digit-naming half without knowing what a digit is and what place it
  occupies. Both skills are independently necessary for this deliverable to be complete.
- **D3 → How to compare two or more values.** The deliverable is a comparison table plus a
  one-paragraph recommendation naming a winner. A student cannot produce a defensible
  recommendation without actually comparing the two designs' computed rates — reporting both rates
  side by side without comparing them (or comparing raw totals instead of the per-square-foot rate)
  fails the deliverable's explicit ask.
