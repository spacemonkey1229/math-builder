## Overview

The incubator room hums at 37 degrees, and Wei Zhang has been staring at the same two vendor
folders for twenty minutes without opening either one. Through the observation window, the pilot
suite sits dark — whichever vessel he recommends this week is the one that gets installed there,
scaled up, and run for a client whose entire production budget for this program lives inside his
line item. His manager's message is still open on his phone: *procurement needs your number by
end of day.*

Wei turns first to the sales sheet for the tank he already knows, the one every operator on his
team could run half-asleep — a straight steel cylinder, predictable, no surprises. Then he opens
the second folder, the one with the glossy cutaway diagram of a flexible bag rocking on its
cradle, its liquid surface curving and flattening with every tilt. He remembers the client's
kickoff call, the words *"we need density, not just volume."* He also remembers the number his
director circled in red on the wave system's quote last quarter, the one that made her wince.

He pulls a legal pad toward him and uncaps a marker. Somewhere in these two folders is a vessel
that actually earns its keep on this run — not the one that sounds better in the sales pitch, but
the one that pencils out. He squares the two folders side by side on the bench, picks up the
first spec sheet, and starts reading the fine print on curvature.

## Background

Wei Zhang is a Bioprocess Engineer at a contract manufacturing organization (CRO) that scales up
client cell-culture processes for production runs. He is responsible for recommending the
production bioreactor for an upcoming batch and accountable to both his director and the client's
program manager for defending that recommendation with numbers, not intuition (SEE APPENDIX A).
Two vessel options are under consideration for this run:

**The Column Bid** — a standard cylindrical stirred-tank bioreactor (STR). Its geometry is simple
and its working volume at any fill level is well characterized by the ordinary cylinder-volume
formula, but its mixing efficiency caps the cell density the batch can reach (SEE APPENDIX B).

**The Wave Bid** — a wave/rocking bioreactor whose flexible bag forms a curved, non-cylindrical
profile as it rocks and fills. Its rocking motion mixes more efficiently and supports a higher
achievable cell density, but because the bag's cross-section is not a simple cylinder, its true
working volume at the batch's target fill level must be found by treating the filled bag as a
solid of revolution generated by its cross-sectional profile, not by assuming the vessel's full
nominal capacity is usable (SEE APPENDIX C).

Both vessels would run the same client cell line under the same feed protocol. The client's
process team has supplied standard growth-kinetics data describing how fast nutrient is fed into
the batch and how fast the culture takes it up over the run (SEE APPENDIX A) — the batch's net
cumulative yield is the surplus that accumulates between those two rates over the full run.

The run is bound by several hard limits. The batch must run for 5 days [MA3], after
which the culture is harvested regardless of vessel. Each vessel must be operated at a target fill
level of 75% (Column Bid) [S5] / 45% (Wave Bid) [S5] of its nominal capacity — cell-culture vessels are never run to the
brim, to leave headspace for gas exchange and foam. The client's program requires a minimum total
yield of 25 L [MA4] of viable product from this run to meet its downstream purification schedule, and
the CRO's program budget caps total vessel and validation spend at
$50,000 [MA5] (SEE APPENDIX D). Wei's recommendation has to clear all three at once.

Getting there requires two calculus techniques Wei hasn't used since a college methods course —
finding the area between two curves and finding the volume of a solid of revolution by the disk
method (SEE APPENDIX F).

All figures in this case are sourced or stated as modeling assumptions in (SEE APPENDIX E).

## Dilemma

Do you:
A.) Recommend the Column Bid — the standard cylindrical stirred-tank bioreactor OR
B.) Recommend the Wave Bid — the wave/rocking bioreactor with the curved bag profile

| Validation cost (USD) | $15,000 [MA1] |

*Derivation of internal diameter and straight-side height [S1]: the XDR-50 datasheet reports a
50 L maximum working volume and an aspect ratio H/D = 2.25:1. Solving V = π(D/2)²H with H = 2.25D
gives D³ = 50,000 cm³ ÷ (0.5625π) ≈ 28,297 cm³, so D ≈ 30.5 cm and H = 2.25D ≈ 68.6 cm.*
