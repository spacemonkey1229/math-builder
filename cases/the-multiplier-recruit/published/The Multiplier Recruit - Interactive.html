Overview

The email from the site-selection consultant landed at 4:52 the night before, and Ben Okafor has read it eleven times since. Both offers are still open. Both close in eighteen hours. On his desk are two folders, each thick with letterhead and site plans, and one number he cannot stop thinking about: the Calder County Industrial Development Authority has exactly one incentive package to hand out this fiscal year, and the board votes at nine tomorrow morning.

To his left, Meridian Forgeworks — a precision-components manufacturer that would buy steel, tooling, and packaging from a dozen local shops Ben already knows by name. To his right, Cascade Distribution — a regional logistics center that would hire more people outright and open a building on the county's biggest vacant industrial lot within the year. His predecessor used to settle calls like this with a gut feeling and a spreadsheet of direct hires. Ben pulls up the regional input-output worksheet instead, the one the board has never once asked him to actually show his work on. Tonight, he thinks, they will.

He opens both employer packets side by side, uncaps a pen, and starts building the matrix.

Background

Ben Okafor is the regional economic development analyst for the Calder County Industrial Development Authority (IDA). He is responsible for recommending which employer the IDA should recruit with the county's single annual incentive package, and accountable to the IDA board for showing that his recommendation is not just a guess (see Appendix A). Two employers have each submitted a formal relocation offer, and the IDA can fund only one:

The Anvil Bid — recruiting Meridian Forgeworks, a precision-components manufacturer that would hire 140 people directly and purchase most of its steel, tooling, and packaging from local suppliers already operating in Calder County (see Appendix A).

The Waypoint Bid — recruiting Cascade Distribution, a regional logistics and distribution center that would hire 310 people directly, more than double Meridian's direct headcount, but would purchase comparatively little from other Calder County businesses once its building and racking are installed (see Appendix A).

Both employers would receive the exact same incentive rate per direct job they create — Calder County's incentive program pays a flat dollar amount per net new full-time job, modeled on a real, publicly published state incentive schedule (see Appendix D). Read only by that headline rate, the two bids are identical: the same dollars per direct job, every time. That is exactly why the headline rate cannot be the number the board votes on.

What the flat per-job rate does not capture is what economists call the regional multiplier effect: money a new employer spends locally on suppliers and services gets spent again by those suppliers' own workers, and again by the next round of local vendors, before it finally leaks out of the county. A manufacturer that buys heavily from local suppliers keeps more of each dollar circulating inside Calder County than a distribution center that buys comparatively little locally — even if the distribution center hires more people directly. The real federal tool for measuring exactly this effect is the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis's Regional Input-Output Modeling System, RIMS II (see Appendix C) — the same tool practicing regional economic developers like Ben actually use, priced per region ordered directly from BEA. Calder County's IDA has never purchased a RIMS II table for its own county, so Ben has to build his own regional model by hand, calibrated to real, published RIMS II multiplier examples (see Appendices B and C), starting from the same kind of county-level baseline employment picture the real U.S. Census Bureau's County Business Patterns program publishes for every U.S. county [S4].

Ben's recommendation must be judged on one number: total regional jobs supported — direct, indirect, and induced — per $10,000 of incentive spent, found by solving Calder County's two-sector regional economy as a system of linear equations using matrices (see Appendices B, D and E). Every sourced and assumed figure in this case is listed in Appendix F, and the vocabulary and “how to…” skills this case will ask of you are listed in Appendix G — that is where Challenge 1 sends you.

Appendix B — Calder County's Regional Technical Coefficient Matrix

Ben's model divides Calder County's economy into two sectors for this decision: Sector 1 — Manufacturing (Meridian Forgeworks's industry) and Sector 2 — Trade, Transportation & Warehousing (Cascade Distribution's industry).

Each entry of the technical coefficient matrix A below is the number of dollars a sector must buy from Calder County businesses — not from outside the region — to produce one additional dollar of its own output. Column 1 is Sector 1's local buying pattern; column 2 is Sector 2's.

What a Type I final-demand multiplier means

For every extra $1 of output a region's final-demand delivers to one industry, the multiplier is the total dollar change in output across every industry in the region once the resulting local supply-chain purchases (but not household spending — that's what makes it “Type I,” not “Type II”) ripple through. A multiplier of 1.3616 means $1.00 of new manufacturing demand produces $1.3616 of total regional output. This is exactly the same idea Appendix B's matrix computes by hand: (I − A)−1 applied to a demand vector is a Leontief-style multiplier calculation, the same family of math behind BEA's own published RIMS II tables.

    Reveal the Twist

    Look back at Appendix D. Both bids pay exactly the same $3,000 per direct job. If the rate is identical, is the county's choice really a wash?

    An identical per-job rate tells you the two bids cost the same for each direct hire. It does not tell you what each employer's hiring does to the rest of Calder County's economy after the hiring happens — and that is exactly what Appendix B's matrix is built to capture. Before you lock in a recommendation, make sure D5's last column — jobs per $10,000 of incentive — is actually the number your pitch is defending, not just D4's raw totals.
