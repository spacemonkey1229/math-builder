## Overview

Aromas of risotto, ribollita, and polenta fill the air of Maggiano's Cucina as founder and chef Victorino prepares for tomorrow's client. Buzz. Buzz. His phone lights up on the counter — two new emails, both catering requests.

Victorino wipes his hands on his apron and opens the first one, then the second. "Oh no," he says to himself. "They're the same day."

Knowing that he and his one employee Faustino can't run two events at once, Victorino leans against the counter, rereading both requests. A wedding. A corporate party. Both real clients, both real money, and only one crew.

He pulls a stool up to the counter and opens his order spreadsheet, the one he built himself the week he left his old accounting job behind. Every menu item, every ingredient cost, every fee he charges is already in there — he just has to actually run the numbers instead of guessing which client feels like the safer bet.

Whichever one he picks, the numbers have to actually work — for his business, and for the family waiting for him upstairs.

## Background

Victorino Maggiano's passion for cooking began in his grandfather's kitchen. Last year he left a corporate accounting job to start catering small events, and after months of scraping by on savings, he took a loan to finance a small 4-oven kitchen in Draper, Utah and hired one employee, Faustino. With only the two of them preparing, delivering, and cleaning up, Maggiano's Cucina can only take one event at a time. The loan payment is **$950 every month**, and Victorino has promised himself that any single event's net profit should be able to cover it on its own.

Two requests have come in for the same day (SEE APPENDIX A): the **Beal wedding**, at the Millennial Falls Event Center, and the **Adobitron corporate party**, at Adobitron HQ. Maggiano's menu, and the cost of each item's ingredients from his food supplier, are in **APPENDIX B**. Every catering order also carries an event fee (staff time, including required prep and cleanup) and a delivery fee, plus a standing food-safety policy: Maggiano's always prepares 7% more servings than the guest count, so the kitchen never runs out mid-event (SEE APPENDIX C).

Weddings and corporate events aren't just a one-time choice, either — Victorino has noticed a real pattern over his first year: corporate clients tend to call back for repeat business (monthly lunches, quarterly parties), while wedding clients are almost always a single booking. Whichever event pays better today, only one of the two client types is likely to become the kind of steady, recurring work Maggiano's Cucina actually needs to survive its second year.

Victorino has to pick one client. The other gets a polite, apologetic email.

## Appendix C: Modeling Assumptions

**Assumption 1 — The 7% buffer.** Maggiano's always prepares 7% more servings than the guest count, so a shortfall never happens mid-event. Round up to the next whole serving.

**Assumption 2 — Delivery distance.** Round-trip driving distance from Maggiano's Cucina (Draper, UT) is approximately **8 miles** to the Millennial Falls Event Center (same city) and approximately **18 miles** to Adobitron HQ (Lehi, UT) — reasonable estimates for the area, not a routed, sourced figure.

## Appendix D: Fees

**Event fee:** $115/hour for Maggiano's Cucina staff on hand, which always includes 1 hour of prep before the event and 1 hour of cleanup after — a 3-hour event is billed for 5 hours total.

**Delivery fee:** $1.65/mile, round trip, to and from Maggiano's Cucina.
