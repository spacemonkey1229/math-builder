# Guide Notes: Catering

**Case:** Catering · **Tier:** T2 · **Band:** MS · **Studio:** Heroes Academy
**Standards:** see `case.json.standards[]` for the exact ids (11 skills — decimals, percent,
proportions, multiplication, equations, and comparison)

Legacy conversion, 2026-08-01, reformatted from the original 2021 Guide Notes
(`source/Y1/Session 4/Catering/Guide Notes.docx`) onto the current 11-anchor ladder. Note for the
facilitator: "Maggiano's Cucina" coincidentally resembles the real national chain Maggiano's Little
Italy — this case's business, founder, and numbers are entirely invented and have no connection to
that real company; worth knowing in case a Hero asks.

## Intro

Frame the format: this is a real small-business decision with real money on both sides, not a
worksheet. Name the opener — "Two clients, one crew, one day. Victorino has to say no to one of
them by tonight."

## Launch

Read both events' guest counts and menus aloud (Appendix A), with no dollar figures computed yet.
Ask: "Just from the guest counts and menus, which event do you think makes more money?" Do not
confirm or correct answers here.

## Anchor 1 — Take a stand and vote

**TAKE A STAND AND VOTE. No reasoning yet, just commit.**

"Who takes the Beal wedding? Who takes the Adobitron party?" Show of hands, no discussion. Record
the count — Close will ask them to vote again.

## Anchor 2 — What is the problem we're solving?

**"Is this the right question?" · "Is it just as simple as which job pays more, or is there
something else to consider?"**

Let confusion grow here, deliberately — this is the original Guide Notes' own move, preserved. Push
on the Background's own hint: Maggiano's is seeing more repeat corporate business than seasonal
weddings. Is "which pays more today" the only real question, or is there a second one about which
client type is worth cultivating?

## Anchor 3 — What is the process?

**"If we go through this process and find the data, how will we decide?"**

Once the room has a working definition of the tradeoff, ask them to name the actual steps: find the
buffered serving count (a proportion), total the revenue and cost, total the fees, subtract to get
net profit. Re-ask: "Do we agree on that process?"

## Anchor 4 — What don't we know?

**"Do we need to make assumptions? Which ones are critical?"**

Surface Appendix C's two real conventions: the 7% buffer rounds up to a whole serving, and the
delivery mileage is a reasonable estimate, not a routed figure. Ask which one actually changes the
final answer if it's off (mileage barely moves net profit; the buffer's rounding rule does more).

## Data Deep Dive

Now, and only now, the numbers. Walk D1's proportion setup and D2-D4's revenue/cost/fee chain
together as a room, cold-calling for the proportion step specifically — this is where a Hero who
just multiplied 1.07 x guests without setting up the proportion will get the same number but miss
the skill the case is actually asking for.

**D5, once D4 is solid.** Victorino's real $950 monthly loan payment (Background) is a genuine bar
to clear — does either event's real booked profit actually cover it? Cold-call for isolating
servings on one side of `margin_per_serving x s - fees = 950`. The real answer is worth sitting
with either way: the wedding, as actually booked (118 servings), needs 123 to clear the loan and
falls $52.58 short; the corporate party, as actually booked (107 servings), needs 122 and falls
$189.38 short. **Neither event alone covers the loan payment as booked.** Ask: "Does that change
how confident anyone is in their pick, or does one event's shortfall matter less than the other's?"

## Twist — drop this at ~15 minutes into the discussion

Deliver slowly, with pauses:

"Whichever event you picked, the client just called. Fifteen more guests are coming to the Beal
wedding — 125 total now, not 110."

**Facilitator-only: what the numbers actually do (`case.json.derived`).** The wedding's net profit
rises from $897.42 to $1,098.86 with the extra 15 guests (134 buffered servings instead of 118).
**The twist does not flip the verdict — the wedding was already ahead, and this widens the gap.** A
Hero who recommended the corporate party now has a real, harder case to make: the gap they were
already arguing against just grew by over $200. Ask: "Does this change your recommendation, or just
make you defend it more carefully?"

## Immediate recommit

Re-vote immediately. Ask by name: "Did the extra 15 guests change anyone's answer? If you were
already on the wedding, does this make your case stronger or does it not actually matter to your
reasoning?"

## Deep conflict — three threads (20-30 min)

1. **One-time profit vs. recurring client.** The wedding profits more today; the corporate party
   represents the client type Maggiano's is actually growing toward. Is a bigger one-time number a
   good reason to walk away from the trend the business itself is pointing at?
2. **Whose risk is bigger.** A 15-guest last-minute change is easy to absorb on paper — is it
   equally easy for a two-person kitchen to actually execute?
3. **What "the numbers work" really means.** Net profit is one number. Reputation, referrals, and
   which client type Victorino wants more of next year aren't in the spreadsheet at all — should
   they be?

## 45-minute Socratic plan

| Time | Move |
|---|---|
| 0:00–3:00 | Launch — read both orders aloud, cold-call for a gut read, no dollar figures yet |
| 3:00–10:00 | Position defense — *"Convince us your event is the better business decision, not just the bigger number"* |
| 10:00–15:00 | Pressure-test the models — *"Whose net-profit number is doing the most work?"* |
| **15:00** | **DROP THE TWIST** — 15 more wedding guests, delivered slowly |
| 15:00–20:00 | Immediate recommit — did anyone's answer actually change |
| 20:00–30:00 | Threaded deep conflict (one-time vs. recurring; whose risk; what the numbers don't capture) |
| 30:00–45:00 | Close, re-vote, reflection, lessons learned |

## The trap

A Hero computes net profit, finds the wedding ahead, and stops — never engaging with the
Background's own signal that corporate events are becoming Maggiano's more consistent business.
The case's real tension is that the bigger number today and the better trend for the business
might point different directions. Watch for a pitch that reports one number and calls the case
closed.

## Socratic questions

1. The wedding nets $897.42 and the corporate party nets $760.62 — a real 17.99% gap. How would you explain to Victorino why that gap is or isn't worth building a recommendation on by itself?
2. The Background says Maggiano's is seeing more corporate repeat business than seasonal weddings — should that fact change today's decision, or is today's decision only about today's numbers?
3. If the delivery distance estimate to Adobitron HQ were off by 10 miles, how much would that actually change the corporate party's net profit?
4. The 7% buffer rounds up to a whole serving — why does that rounding rule matter more at a small guest count than a large one?
5. After the twist, the wedding's lead grew by over $200 — does that make the decision easier, or does it just make whichever choice you already made feel more or less right?

## Close

**VOTE AGAIN.** "Did we ask the right question at the start?" "What math was new to you tonight, and
how did you actually attack it?" Compare the closing vote to Anchor 1's — a room that didn't move at
all after the twist is worth asking about directly: did the extra guests not matter, or did the room
already expect the wedding's lead to hold?

## Lessons Learned

*(To be filled after the first real run — placeholder structure only.)*

- Whether the proportion setup (D1) or the multi-step fee subtraction (D4) was the place most
  Heroes actually got stuck.
- Whether Heroes engaged with the recurring-client-type argument on their own or needed Anchor 2's
  prompt to surface it.
- Whether the +15-guest twist felt like a meaningful reveal or an expected, minor adjustment.

**A real, disclosed instability, for a facilitator who gets pushed on it:** Appendix C, Assumption
2's delivery-mileage estimates are not routed or sourced — that is stated plainly in the case text.
If a Hero (or the room) asks "what if the corporate mileage estimate is off," the honest answer is
that it genuinely matters: flexing `corporate_miles` (18 miles, Adobitron HQ) up by 50% (to 27
miles) pushes the case's own margin from 17.99% to 20.3%, past this project's 20% balance cap —
`wedding_miles` and the 7% buffer rate both stay stable across the same ±50% range, so this is
specific to the corporate estimate, not the case's math in general. There is no more-precise real
number to look up here — Adobitron is a fictional company invented for this case, so there is no
real address to route a distance against. This is disclosed, not eliminated: it carries a
Category-F `case.json.sensitivity_disclosure[]` entry. If pressed, tell the room the honest thing —
the wedding's real lead over the corporate party (17.99%, before any twist) is genuine but not
enormous, and part of why it's not enormous is a delivery-distance estimate that could reasonably
have been drawn a little differently.
