Overview

Devon Reyes has the cutting table lights on at 6:00 in the morning, a bolt of canvas unrolled to its full length, and a boutique's rush order sitting on the clipboard: forty bib aprons, ready for pickup Thursday, no exceptions.

Two marker layouts are chalked out side by side on the table's paper backing. One uses the fold — half the pattern pieces, half the tracing, half the pinning, a shortcut Devon has used a hundred times before. The other lays every piece flat and open, packed edge to edge like a jigsaw puzzle nobody asked for, but using less cloth to do it, which is exactly the kind of number the shop owner watches.

Devon's boss sticks her head in the cutting room, coffee in hand, coat still on. “Whichever marker you cut from is the one we're stuck with. Fabric's not cheap, but neither is your morning — and I've got a client meeting in twenty minutes who's going to ask which one we ran.”

Devon picks up the tailor's chalk, looks at the bolt, and does the math one more time before committing a single cut.

Background

Devon Reyes cuts and marks fabric at a small custom-apparel shop, responsible for laying out pattern pieces on stock fabric before a single cut is made — and for knowing which layout actually costs less to run, not just which one looks more efficient on the table.

The job: 40 bib aprons, cut from cotton canvas sold on a 44-inch-wide bolt (Appendix A). Every seam uses the industry-standard ⊥-inch seam allowance — ⅝-inch, to be exact (Appendix B). Two marker layouts are on the table.

Cut on the Fold places the apron's body panel with one edge on a folded double layer of fabric, so tracing and pinning one half automatically produces a mirrored whole panel. It uses 1¼ yards of fabric per apron and takes 3 minutes of marking time per apron — fewer pieces to place and trace (Appendix C).
Cut Open lays every piece fully flat, individually nested tighter against its neighbors on the bolt. It uses only 1⅙ yards of fabric per apron, but takes 6 minutes of marking time per apron — more pieces to place, and every one has to be matched against its mirror-image partner by hand (Appendix C).

Every yard of fabric costs the same regardless of layout (Appendix A). Every minute of marking labor costs the same regardless of which layout it goes toward (Appendix D). The total job cost is yards used × fabric price, plus minutes used × labor rate — two line items that move in opposite directions as the layout changes.

Before either layout was chalked out, Devon also had to work out the apron's own real dimensions — the waist ties, the trimmed marker length, how many aprons a full bolt actually yields — all of it in the fractions and mixed numbers a tape measure and a fabric bolt actually come in, not tidy decimals.

    • D2 — The trimmed marker length: a raw marker segment measures 36⅝ inches; both ends need 1⅜ inches trimmed off to clear a damaged selvedge. Find the usable length after both trims.
    • D3 — Aprons per bolt: a full bolt holds 22½ yards. Using Cut on the Fold's yardage per apron, find how many aprons one full bolt yields.
    • D4 — Splitting a remnant: a ⅝-yard remnant is left over and needs to be split evenly into 5 equal pocket-lining pieces. Find the length of each piece.

    • D6 — The hem check: the apron's bottom hem facing is 5 inches deep. Find how many ⅝-inch seam allowances fit inside that 5-inch depth.
    • D7 — A total cost verdict: using Appendix A's fabric price and Appendix D's labor rate, find the total cost of the 40-apron order under each layout — material plus labor — state the dollar difference and percentage margin, and recommend.

    Reveal the Twist

    “It's the middle of the discussion. The boutique client calls back. They've settled on the fabric — a directional canvas print, an arrow pattern that has to run the same way up on every piece.”

    “A one-way print like that can force a fold-cut layout to use extra fabric, since the fold can leave some pieces running against the print's direction unless the marker is repositioned to fix it. Cut Open, laid out fully flat and open, isn't affected the same way. Does Cut on the Fold still come out ahead once you price that in?”
